## Done

- **The handedness question is answered, and it was the wrong question.** None of
  the three candidate explanations is true. `n` is measured from the **hip**, and
  on an edge the hip is not above the blade: at hipZ 86, n = +17.2 is an 11.8°
  lean. Frame 60 is a deepening forward outside edge, not crossed feet, and the
  free foot said to be crossing it is 70 cm away along the track. `model.md` is
  right, the entry data is right, and comparing two feet by their `n` alone is the
  move that produced the phantom.
- **A real defect, found on the way past.** The skating blade must sit on the
  **outside** of its lobe. Every keyframe in all three moves does, except the six
  waltz **landing** keys, which had the skater leaning out of the landing circle
  by 6–11°. Three independent routes agree: sign of `n` against `lobeSense`; the
  biting edge from the edge letter, measured against `hipYaw`; and the drawn
  top-down hip, 22 cm outside the landing circle where the entry's sits 19 cm
  inside. Fixed by hand — the six `n` values negated, nothing else touched.
- **`tools/lean.mjs`, the eighth checker.** Both routes, per frame not per
  keyframe, 922 frames on an edge across the three moves. It was written before
  the fix and failed on the live bug rather than on a mutation. `--break` puts the
  old landing back and it fails again.
- **The side view's camera is settled and `NEARER.side` is `q => q.n`.** The
  along-track axis plots +t to the right so a skater going forwards reads left to
  right; that puts the camera on the skater's right, at +n. The old value ordered
  depth from the skater's **left** while the boot's toe/heel test twenty lines
  below drew from their **right**. Both now read one expression, so that pair
  cannot drift apart again.
- **`tools/frame-svg.mjs`.** Any frame of any view as a standalone SVG, with no
  browser — the renderers only need a DOM to hang elements off, so it gives them a
  stub and walks the tree back out. Same code path as the page.
- **Docs current.** `model.md` gained the lean section and the correction that
  "+n is the skater's right" is a mnemonic for a forward pose, not a definition;
  `state-of-play.md` gained the finding and lost the resolved SIDE-casing caveat;
  `design-brief.md` gained where the cameras stand.
- Eight checkers pass: `harness reach shin blade freefoot lean tracing contrast`.

**Then twizzles**, deferred since Session 01 and picked up once the arms went to design.

- **The deferral was right, for a sharper reason than was written down.** A twizzle breaks
  the model's founding axiom: everywhere else a blade points along its own tracing, but a
  tangent can only wind in whole turns, so the extra half of a 1½ is not in the tracing at
  all. It is the body turning against the blade — the skid.
- **BIS's own definition is geometry twice over.** "A series of checked three turns is not
  acceptable, as this does not constitute a continuous action" is cusps against curls;
  "if the travelling stops, it becomes a solo spin" is the advance going to zero.
- **The flags are computed, not typed.** Curls cannot curve against themselves, so the lobe
  always continues; a half rotation turns the skater round, so the direction reverses; the
  lobe continuing then forces the edge letter to flip with it. LFI 1½ comes out LBO, and
  the blade never changed which way it curved.
- `TWIZZLES` in `skating.js` — one key per rotation count, because the count decides the
  exit and `exitState` has to be able to see it. Single, 1½, double.
- The tracing is a trochoid in `edge-diagram.js`. Advance under the curl radius, or the
  tangent never completes its turn and the curve scallops; ratio 0.55, chosen by Martyn
  from four rendered candidates.
- `tracing.mjs` gained five twizzle assertions, now 184 combinations. Broken on purpose:
  advance 1.4 → 72 failures, advance 0 → 24, advance 0.98 → 24 caught as a cusp.
- **Twenty-four new elements**, eight entry edges by three rotation counts, prose keyed on
  entry edge and count. 156 elements → 180. `kind: twizzle`, and a chip-row section on
  `/elements/` modelled on the transitions one.

## Decisions locked

- **`n` is a track axis and reads as lean first.** "+ = the skater's right" holds
  only while the skater faces the way they are going. The body-relative direction
  is `lateral(hipYaw)` and always was.
- **The skating blade sits outside its lobe, and the body leans over the biting
  edge.** Two claims from different inputs that must agree on every frame.
- **A camera side is a decision, not a fact.** It cannot be checked, so what is
  checked instead is that everything in a panel reads it off the same line. Side:
  camera at +n. Rear: camera at −t.
- **A twizzle's flags are computed from its rotation count.** One key per count in
  `TWIZZLES`, not one key plus a number on the element: the count decides the exit,
  and `exitState` reads flags and nothing else. Storing it as element data would put
  a fact that changes the exit somewhere the model cannot see it.
- **A twizzle is drawn on a lobe**, at a curl-to-advance ratio of 0.55. Martyn's call
  from four rendered candidates, 29/08.
- **A pose fix is a hand fix.** The six landing keys were flipped one at a time
  against an expected value, and `reach`, `shin`, `blade`, `freefoot` and
  `tracing` re-run after. A sign flip does not change a distance, which is exactly
  why those five had nothing to say about the bug in the first place.

## Open questions — twizzles

- **Does the tracing look like that on real ice?** The curl-to-advance ratio was picked
  from rendered candidates, not from a rink. The Skills 3 video is the forward inside 1½.
- **Does the blade really stay on one physical edge throughout?** The model says the letter
  changes only because the skater turns round, and that the blade passes through flat at
  the quarter points. A derivation, not an observation, and the single claim in the twizzle
  pages most worth a coach's eye.
- **Should a twizzle sit on a lobe?** Martyn's call, 29/08: yes, as drawn. Worth revisiting
  if the lobe makes a twizzle read as a loop at phone size.

## Open questions — the rig

- **Is 11° deep enough for a waltz landing?** The lean is now on the correct side
  and matches the entry's depth. Whether a landing should be *deeper* than its
  entry is a skater's judgement; the geometry has no opinion.
- **Are the poses right at all?** Standing since Session 01. `verified: false`
  still applies to all of it. This session moved one pose from provably wrong to
  not provably wrong, which is not the same as right.
- **Is 1.8:1 enough weight separation in a rink?** Unchanged from Session 02 — a
  person with a phone in a cold rink.
- **Should the airborne state say which foot is about to take the weight?**
  Unchanged from Session 02.

## Prerequisites

- **Playwright cannot be installed from the device bridge.** `cdn.playwright.dev`
  is refused by the bridge VM's allowlist, so `check:ink`, `check:framing`,
  `check:speed` and `check:offline` cannot run from a Cowork session at all. Run
  them from a terminal on the Mac. `tools/frame-svg.mjs` exists because of this
  and covers geometry, not CSS or layout.
- **`npm run build` fails in the bridge VM.** `node_modules` is installed for
  macOS and the bridge shell is Linux, so rolldown's native binding is missing.
  Nothing to fix in the repo; `npm run check` must finish on the Mac. Everything
  before `npm run build` in that chain does run here.
- Git is still run by the human. Read-only commands left no `.lock` behind this
  session, but the convention stands.

Two things to delete rather than commit, and neither can be deleted from the
bridge — `device_bash` has no `rm`:

- **`_to_delete/index.lock.stale`** is `.git/index.lock`, moved out of the way. A
  `git status` run through the bridge leaves one behind and cannot remove it; left
  in place it blocks every subsequent write. This is the `.lock` problem Session 01
  recorded, caught and defused rather than avoided.
- **`shots/lean-evidence/`** is this session's before-and-after dumps. Evidence, not
  output; nothing references it.

Note that `git add -A` would commit both, which is why the second commit above
lists paths instead.
