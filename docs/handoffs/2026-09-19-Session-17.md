## The two two-foot turns needed no new capability, only the cost of a moving yaw

The skid and the per-foot yaw landed in Session 16 with no element using either. These are
the first. Everything they needed existed — and every skid before them was a held stop at
one fixed angle, so these are the first poses in the file whose yaw *moves*. That is where
the whole session's work came from.

**A turn is a yaw sweeping through a half circle, and `dir` never changes.** That reads
backwards, because the skater ends up gliding backwards and there is a field that says so.
It cannot be used: `dir` is a carried state and `yaw` an interpolated quantity, so flipping
`dir` partway takes 180° off the base and sends the yaw back through zero to compensate.
Every frame between those two keyframes would draw the blades swinging the wrong way and
then back — the lpP failure of Session 16 in a new costume. So the whole rotation lives in
the yaw, which on a skid is exact rather than a convention: a skidding blade has no line of
its own to travel along, which is the whole of what `onIce: 'skid'` says.

**The rig starts and ends mid-skid, at 25° and 155° of body rotation.** That span is not
trimmed for looks, it is the skid floor read from both ends. A skid must be turned past
`SKID_MIN_YAW`, a blade running true is not a skid, and `yaw` interpolates continuously —
so there is no arrangement of keyframes that goes from a true-running blade to a skidding
one without frames in between that are a yawed blade claiming to grip. The rig draws the
turn and not the glide into it, the way `twoFoot` does not draw stepping onto two feet.

**The pivot is on the reference blade, and that is the rig's shape showing through.** The
reference blade is pinned to the path and the hip hangs off it, so a reference foot whose
hip-relative position rotates makes the HIP orbit the tracing by the stance width, four
times life in the plan view. A skater swerving half a metre sideways is a worse picture
than a pivot placed a stance-width off centre. It also lands where an anticlockwise turn
would put it, so the licence and the movement agree rather than merely not colliding.

**The rise is the technique and the shoulders lead.** `hipZ` 94-97-94, because a skater
rises through the middle of a turn to take weight off the blades; `shYaw` 25° ahead of
`hipYaw` at the first key and level at the last, which is the element's own sentence about
winding the upper body written as numbers. Arms gathered to 46 cm rather than the default
67 — a wide carriage swung through 180° is the fore-and-aft sweep the waltz jump's note
already warns about.

**The edges are the weakest thing here.** Both blades are near flat and the letters say
which side of flat: rotating anticlockwise puts the skater fractionally over the left of
both boots, so the left is outside and the right inside. Two independent arguments give the
same answer (the lean, and the guide's own one-outside-one-inside rule for two blades), and
`lean.mjs` does not hold a skid to either. It is a record of a tilt, not a load-bearing
number, and it belongs on the coach list.

## Two faults fell out of it, and no checker could see either

**`turnout.mjs` read the skid floor off nought only.** `|yaw| < SKID_MIN_YAW`, so a blade at
176° — running as true along its own line as one at 4°, backwards — passed by a mile. No
pose could reach that while the only skids in the file were three held stops at 40, 45 and
90 degrees; the branch had never met the case. This repository's own rule for the fourth
time: an exemption can only excuse a pose, so assert from both sides.

**And the scrape was drawn as a round-capped stroke.** One stroke carries one width, so the
band broke into ten-degree buckets — fine for a held stop, which is one bucket and one
stroke, and useless for a sweeping yaw, which bucketed into thirteen short round-capped
strokes of rising width: a caterpillar of overlapping discs where the mark should taper.
Every checker was green. Found by looking, which is the third time a glyph has been wrong in
a case it had never met. The band is a filled shape now, its half-width read off each
frame's own yaw, offset along each point's own normal.

**And the hash corrected the note I wrote about it.** `tools/frame-svg.mjs` over all sixteen
moves at seven times, 336 frames, before and after: **24 moved, all of them the top view of
four moves** — `snowplough`, `ploughBack` and the two turns. The first draft of the comment
said five and counted the T-stop. The T-stop did not move: its skid is the *trailing* blade,
and the mark is the reference blade's, which on a T-stop is gliding. **Only a skidding
reference blade leaves a band.** Measure the claim, including the one in your own comment.

A procedural note for the next session, learned the hard way: the baseline render was
started and then the renderer was patched while it was still running, which spoiled it. If
the before-and-after hash matters, `git stash push -- <the file>` for the baseline run.

## The spins needed nothing at all

Outstanding since Session 14, the largest single item in `docs/gaps-competition.md`, which
called it "a rig project, not a content one". **The rig project was finished on 30/08/2026
and nobody went back for the content.** The three moves had been sitting in `moves.js`
marked PROBE, drawn on `/rig`, with no page pointing at them, and `tools/spin.mjs` had been
holding them against the ISU's own definitions per frame the whole time.

So: one page each for upright, sit and camel, mounted on those rigs. The hub grows a Spins
card and `/elements/in/spin/` builds itself, because `elementGroups` derives its sections
from the kinds present rather than listing them — `SECTION_NOTE` is the one line written by
hand, which is exactly why that map is keyed on the generated section id.

The three `note` strings lost the word PROBE. A move a reader arrives at is not a probe,
whatever it was built as.

Prose carries shape and purpose in our words per `docs/style.md`, with one sourced number
each: the ISU's three-revolution minimum, the thigh-parallel line for the sit, and
free-knee-above-hip for the camel — that last one worth stating because the definition is a
knee height and not the flat horizontal line most people picture.

**Both `gaps-competition.md` and `state-of-play.md` still said the guide held no spins.**
Corrected, with what is still missing and which half of it is model work.

## Decisions locked

- **A two-foot turn's rotation lives entirely in `yaw`; `dir` does not change mid-move.**
  Forced by `dir` carrying while `yaw` interpolates. Applies to any future move that turns
  on the ice.
- **A rig may start and end mid-skid.** The skid floor cuts both ends off a turn, and
  drawing the entry would mean authoring frames the model does not have a state for. Same
  family as `toePick`'s undrawn entry and `twoFoot`'s undrawn step-on.
- **The pivot of an on-ice rotation sits on the reference blade**, to keep the hip on the
  tracing. A modelling licence, stated in the move's comment.
- **`SKID_MIN_YAW` is asserted from both 0 and 180.** Amends the Session 16 decision that
  set the floor, which asserted one side only.
- **The scrape band is a filled shape, not a stroke**, with per-frame half-width. Changes
  the appearance of `snowplough` and `ploughBack` by half a blade at each end, deliberately.
- **The three spin moves are elements, not probes.**
- **Spin coverage stops at the three basic positions this session** (Martyn's call, asked
  and answered mid-session). Everything above them is listed in `gaps-competition.md`.
