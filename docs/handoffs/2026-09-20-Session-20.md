## The step pass closed eleven lines, not nine

Nine `notCovered` entries carry the literal phrase *step wide*. Two more, in Skills 2 exercise
3 and Skills 3 exercise 3, describe the same manoeuvre without it. Eleven.

**BIS define "step" twice and the two are incompatible**, which is why this needed a decision
rather than an afternoon's authoring. Their *Definition of Steps, Turns & Movements* opens
taxonomically — *the visible tracing on the ice that is executed on one foot* — under which
every edge and one-foot turn in the guide is already a step. The same document then names four
elements in the other sense, by **where the free foot is put down**: slip step, toe step,
crossed step in front, crossed step behind. And this repository had a third: `STEPS` in
`skating.js` is the mohawk and the choctaw, which BIS call *a turn from one foot to the other*.

**The cut is geometric, not lexical,** and that is the decision. Making `kind: 'step'` BIS's
placement family and moving the crossed step behind and the slip chassé into it was the
tempting answer and is wrong: the same list names the cross roll and both chassés in the same
register without the word, so *crossed step behind* is one name rather than *crossed* plus
*step*. Cutting on which names happen to contain an English word builds a kind out of
vocabulary, and re-slugs shipped pages to do it. So `element-groups.js`'s existing sentence
stands and is load-bearing now: **a transition is how you get from one edge to the next; a step
is a way of moving that changes neither.** The slip step keeps `kind: 'step'` to itself, and
the spread eagle and the Ina Bauer are the shape of its future.

**Nothing was chosen about the flags.** Read off BIS's own sequences, all nine agree without
exception: a step wide is **inside edge to inside edge**, which is the cross roll's flag set,
and a push back is **inside to outside**, which is the crossover's. `exitState` produces every
destination in the syllabus unaltered.

So why were eleven lines unlinked for eleven sessions? `gen-derived.mjs` restricts the
crossover, the chassé and the cross roll to **outside** entries, correctly, with the note that
it is what the elements are rather than a modelling limitation. True of those three, and it
left the inside half of both flag sets generated by nobody. The syllabus lives in that half.

`TRANSITION_ENTRIES` widened from an edge letter to a **direction and an edge**, because the
push back is skated backwards and never forwards in any of the eight tests. The seven existing
entries are the same sets said in a wider alphabet, so no shipped page moved; both readers were
updated with it. Six generated pages, no model change, **0 of 441 hashed frames moved** —
stashed and re-rendered rather than assumed.

A push back draws what a backward stroke draws, because it is the same push. It is a transition
rather than a basic for the reason `forward-stroking` is a basic while `lfo-chasse` is a
transition though a chassé contains a push: a basic is how you push at all, a transition is
which edge you leave and which you arrive on.

**Two gaps survive, narrowed rather than deleted.** Skills 2 exercise 3 steps from a backward
outside edge onto a forward outside one, changing foot and facing with no turn, and BIS name no
step that arrives the other way round. Skills 8 section 2 keeps its push or touch down.

## `rig:` names a real move, and it was not only a tripwire

Twenty minutes as billed, and it found a live hole on the way. `BodyFrame.astro` wraps its
figure in `{m && (...)}`, so a `rig:` naming nothing renders **nothing**: no figure, no warning,
a green build. `drawn.mjs` catches that only on a page whose sole picture is the rig, which is
the blind spot its own header confesses to, one field along.

Asserted from both sides. A page may not name a move that does not exist; and a move that
exists must be named by a page or be declared in `unpublished` with a reason that goes stale
visibly. The second is the one with teeth — it is what would have said out loud that the teapot
was rigged in Session 01 with nothing pointing at it, and that `spiralCheck` has been in the
same state since Session 06. Eighteen pages carry a `rig:`, not the sixteen Session 19 said.

Mutations: ghost 2, orphan 1, stale 1. The ghost counts twice because a typo trips both
directions at once.

## Martyn on the waltz landing, and what a default cost

The afternoon started with the two runs `underice.mjs` had been parked beside `drift` for. They
were not a pose nobody was ready to move. They were a pose nobody had asked a coach about.

**Martyn: landing a waltz jump the free foot is not pointed. It is pushed back and NEUTRAL** —
which is what leaves the skater able to step forward onto it and keep moving, or spike the toe
in for the next element; a learner should be shown neither, and the neutral foot leaves both
available. **Through the move it is neutral too**, where it provides the momentum.

`ANKLE_POINT` is 10°, applied to every unauthored free foot, and `rig-math.js` records against
it in as many words: *Verified against a coach: NO.* Both it and `ANKLE_MAX` are read off a
study of ankle injury, which is not a study of what a position looks like. Ten degrees is not
neutral; it is a tenth of the way to a pointed foot applied to every pose nobody had an opinion
about, and on a landing plantarflexion drives the blade at the ice.

`NEUTRAL = 0` sits beside `POINTED = 25` and is authored per key. **`ANKLE_POINT` was not
touched**, because `rig-math.js`'s own finding is that the constant was never the lever: one
value lifts the toe on a spiral and drives it at the ice on a landing. Eighteen keys on the
waltz took `underice` from six runs to two. No foot moved a millimetre; only the ankle did.

**Then one more field.** The waltz's touchdown key is `R:P(-4,11,1,3)` — pitch 3, `point` left
to default. On that key the foot is the SKATING foot, so `point` is a no-op: `bootDir` returns
from the tracing branch before it is read. But the free frames descending into that key
interpolate toward it, so an unwritten 10° drove the arriving toe down 9.6° in the frame before
touchdown. **A value that does nothing where it is written and is load-bearing where nobody
looked.** `NEUTRAL` on it took the waltz to zero, and `underice`'s own `STALE` assertion fired
to say the entry no longer described anything — the both-sides exemption written an hour
earlier catching its first real case.

**Thirty-one free-foot keys across six moves are still on the default**: `extendedEdge` 3,
`teapot` 1, `uprightSpin` 7, `sitSpin` 4, `changeFootSpin` 10, `combinationSpin` 6. Every one
is a foot at a tenth of a point because nobody had an opinion, not because a position asked for
it. The held positions are the ones worth a coach's eye first.

## `underice.mjs` is in the chain

The debt is paid and the exemption is one declared case rather than a pile of special pleading.
Both remaining runs were the same fault: a free foot in the last frames before it takes the
weight. `arriving` holds it from both sides — deeper fails, vanished fails, and a run outside
the window or on the other foot is not excused at all. The windows run from the last key where
the foot is unambiguously in the air to the key where it takes the weight, because the window
is a claim too.

**The depths are measured, not chosen**, and two attempts to deepen them by moving the pose are
recorded in the header because they say what the numbers are: dropping the declared feet a
centimetre does not move them at all, and pointing them to `ANKLE_MAX` moves one by a tenth
while burying four other stretches of the waltz. So no pose mutation can show the comparison
working, and both new mutations break the **declaration** instead — which is what
`--break=stale` already did in `drawn.mjs`.

`npm run underice` is `npm run check:underice` now; the three live references moved with it and
the Session 19 handoff keeps the old name, being a record of what was true then.

## The question that had three answers

Martyn: *there doesn't seem to be any reason why this would need to end up beneath the ice.*
He was right, and the first framing — that it needed the handover modelled — was too quick.

**First answer: the arriving feet were pointed.** Fixed above.

**Second answer: `twofoot.mjs`'s clearance assertion runs on keyframes**, and the file labels
that itself — `── 1, 2, 3: the keyframes ──`. Per frame:

| move | per-key minimum | per-frame minimum | frames under 5 cm |
|---|---|---|---|
| waltz | 10.00 cm | 1.23 cm | 2 |
| changeFootSpin | 14.00 cm | 0.01 cm | 17 |

Every keyframe clears at twice the bound and the feet reach a millimetre between them.
`freefoot.mjs`'s old fault in another file, and those are the only two moves in the guide where
it happens — which is why they were exactly the two `underice` was reporting. **Two checkers
seeing one hole from opposite sides.**

**And the obvious repair is wrong.** Per frame the assertion would be false: a foot being put
down has to cross that band. Five centimetres is a claim about a foot that is STAYING free, and
the rig could not say which kind it was looking at. That is the missing thing, exactly, and
`moves.js` had already diagnosed it twice on 30/08/2026 — *the frames between "free" and
"picked" are a state the model has not got, the foot is neither hanging nor planted*, and
*twoFoot does not draw stepping onto two feet either, for the same reason.*

Specified in `docs/model.md` before being built, on the roll's and the pick's precedent, then
marked up. **Derived, not authored**: a free foot is arriving when the next key is one where it
takes a contact, which `poseAt` already holds. A flag was rejected for the reason `pick: true`
was — a flag exempts and holds nothing to account. `bootDir` blends an arriving boot's direction
toward the planted construction over the last `CLEAR` centimetres, reaching it at contact where
the two agree. The window is a height and not a span of clock: the change of foot descends 16 cm
over 0.14 of its duration and at 16 cm the foot is plainly free.

Four of five predictions held. `boot.mjs` still reports **0.00°** across 26,952 glyphs with no
renderer change, which is the roll's central claim paying out a second time; `continuity.mjs`
stayed green; `freefoot.mjs` needed no exclusion, which was the one thing written down as
unknown; and `underice` went from 0.1 cm over two frames to **0.027 cm over one**.

**Third answer, and it is the one left open.** The fifth prediction said nineteen frames would
move. Thirty-seven are in the window, because I counted arrivals and the specification says
arriving OR departing — and the eighteen departures led somewhere.

**The departure half is built and inert.** A departing foot never reaches `bootDir`'s free
branch, because `onIceOf` answers *on the ice* for anything that is `pose.skate`, and `poseAt`
carries `skate` from the LEFT key. `onIceOf`'s own header warns about that exact conflation and
says the two questions stopped being the same on 30/08/2026. They are still not. Measured per
frame, a blade **claimed on the ice** is drawn:

| move | per-key max | per-frame max | frames over the 3 cm bound |
|---|---|---|---|
| waltz | 2.00 cm | **29.99 cm** | 6 |
| changeFootSpin | 0.00 cm | **15.99 cm** | 33 |

Thirty centimetres in the air, with the tracing built from it, every keyframe passing at nought
to two. Third instance of the same keyframe blindness in one file, and a bigger fault than the
one the section set out to fix.

## Decisions locked

- **A step is geometric, not lexical.** A transition gets you from one edge to the next; a step
  changes neither. The step wide and the push back are transitions; `kind: 'step'` keeps the
  slip step alone.
- **A step wide is I→I and a push back is I→O**, read off the sequences, generated from the
  inside entries only.
- **A free foot is not pointed unless the position says so.** Martyn. `NEUTRAL` per key;
  `ANKLE_POINT` untouched.
- **The arrival is derived from the keys**, never authored.
- **The blend's window is a height, not a span of clock**, and `CLEAR` moved to `rig-math.js`
  because a checker's constant cannot be load-bearing in the model.
- **A hand-written claim about the model throws rather than degrading.** `rig-names.mjs` is the
  fourth file to adopt it.
