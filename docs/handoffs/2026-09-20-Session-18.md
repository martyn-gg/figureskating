## The framing fault: measured from the hip, drawn from the path

`viewTop` pads its box by the furthest marker's offset **from the hip**. But it does not
put the hip on the tracing — it puts the **skating blade** there and hangs the hip off it
at minus that blade's own offset. So every marker is drawn at its hip offset *plus* the
blade's, and the padding was short by the blade's offset in each axis.

Invisible while that offset was lean, a dozen centimetres, which the padding had slack
for. A **snowplough stop is two blades turned forty degrees apart**, which puts the
skating blade a long way to one side of the hip: the foot marker landed 17 px outside the
panel in every frame of it, and 18 in its backward twin.

The fix is the renderer's own `hip = at(-sk.t,-sk.n)` and `rel(q)` read back as a
distance, rather than a guess at what they come to. **It moves only top views** — 91
frames across nine moves plus the spins, with every side and rear view byte-identical.

I got this wrong once first, on a plausible theory that the joint dot's screen-unit radius
outgrew a constant padding. It never fires, and I took the change back out: an inert
branch with a long comment explaining itself is the clutter this file keeps a list of.

`framing.mjs`'s own header said **"3 rig moves x 3 views"** while the loop iterated every
move in the file. Eighteen now. A description of something that changed, for the fifth
time; the count is derived.

## The spins were rebuilt, all five

Martyn, mid-session: **a spin needs an entrance and an exit, and bringing the arms in
increases the speed.** Both were missing from all three. The rigs began at "Rotation
established", ended "Held", and turned at one rate from the first frame to the last —
which draws the *result* of a spin and never the doing of it, and leaves the arms
motionless while they are the thing a beginner is trying to learn.

**Two things the path already carried made it nearly free.** Segments may now differ in
`radius` (two lines in `buildPath`), so the entrance genuinely spirals in. And a segment's
sweep divided by its span has always been its rotation rate, so a wind-up that turns
faster is a real change the animation plays. Both optional and defaulted; **336 frames
hashed before and after prove every move that does not use them draws byte-identically.**

**`at(rate, seg)` makes the rate the authored number and the span the derived one** —
revolutions per second is what a coach says out loud, and spans written in seconds make
`duration` their sum instead of a second opinion. `spinMove` also **snaps a key to a
segment boundary it is within half a frame of**, because where the path changes foot is a
fraction derived from every span in the move, and authoring it by hand was copying a
number the code already knows. Being one part in a million short of it put the pose's
blade and the tracing's blade on different frames for exactly one frame.

**Centred now has an exact meaning:** the blade's lateral offset from the hip equals the
path radius. Everywhere else those are two free numbers, the offset being lean and the
radius the lobe; in a spin they are one. So the entrance is not a separate idea bolted on,
it is the radius coming down to meet the lean, and the spin begins where they meet.
Asserted, and asserted from the other side too — the first segment must **not** be centred,
because a spin that begins centred has no entrance. Only the lateral half is judged: a sit
spin's hip sits a third of a metre behind the blade and whether the free leg balances it is
a question about mass, which this rig does not have.

**The change of foot needed nothing at all.** `lobeSense(L,I,B)` and `lobeSense(R,O,B)` are
both +1, so an arc on the left back inside edge and one on the right back outside edge laid
end to end at the same radius continue **the same circle about the same centre**. The ISU:
"if the spinning centers before and after the change of foot are too far apart ... only the
part before the change of foot will be called". Here they cannot be. Three revolutions each
side is the handbook's number, not a choice, and `spin.mjs` asserts it.

**Spins open at half speed.** Martyn: a spin is worth running slower so a skater sees the
movements rather than the results. `speed` on the move; `RATES` moved into `body-frame.js`
so the server-rendered button label and the client cycle cannot disagree. The component's
own comment has said "the whole reason this rig exists is to be watched slowly" since it
was written, and it opened every move at 1x.

## What the rig cannot do, found by measuring rather than by failing

The camel's free leg would not swing. I misdiagnosed it twice as a fold before measuring
the elevation surface, which says something else entirely:

| free foot | free boot elevation |
|---|---|
| in front, low | +1° |
| behind, low | −52° |
| **behind, mid height** (`t` 0–60, `z` 30–70) | **−67° to −85°, at every reach** |
| behind, high — the held camel | −47° |
| over the hip, high | −62°, between neighbours at +44° and +18° |

At the same 78% of reach the boot reads −4° in front of the hip and −70° behind it, so it
is not a fold a longer leg fixes. Descending at positive `t` is blocked by the bad region;
descending at negative `t` has to cross the free foot over the hip at height, which is the
glyph degeneracy already on the books. **Blocked going up and blocked coming down.**

So the camel spin's rig starts and ends already in the camel, and its entrance and wind-up
are the path tightening and the arms gathering, which is the rest of what they are. Same
family as `twoFoot` not drawing the step-on. And **the combination spin is sit into
upright**, not camel-sit-upright: the ISU permits two basic positions and scores three
higher, and drawing three would mean drawing a change nothing could skate. Both pages say
what is missing and why. Full write-up in `docs/model.md`.

## Broken on purpose

`tools/spin.mjs`, rewritten — the old `isSpin` was `path.length === 1`, true of every spin
on the day it was written and about to stop checking the first spin with an entrance. It
names the property now: every segment an arc, all curving the same way, a full turn in
total. Verified against the file before being loosened — it catches the same moves and
nothing else.

| mutation | reported |
|---|---|
| `--break=flat` every segment's radius set to the move's | 5 |
| `--break=nocentre` the centred offset moved 4 cm off the radius | 5 |
| `--break=short` the change of foot's second side cut to 2 revs | 1 |
| `--break=onepos` the combination's positions all relabelled camel | 7 |
| `--break=windup` the final wind-up relabelled as a position | 4 |
| `--break=foot` the pose's skate held on the left through the change | 1 |
| `--break=entered` the entrance's first segment centred | 5 |

`tools/gather.mjs`, **the twenty-second checker**. The expectation is angular momentum,
from outside the rig, and the ISU's own "clear increase of speed" Level feature.

| mutation | reported |
|---|---|
| `--break=flat` every centred segment at one rate | 5 |
| `--break=wide` the wind-up's arms held at entrance width | 8 |
| `--break=invert` the centred rates reversed | 9 |
| `--break=freespin` the change of foot given a rate rise | 2 |

**It asserts the direction and not the ratio.** A real skater's moment of inertia falls
three or four times between wide arms and crossed ones because the mass is in a trunk that
barely moves; a marker model has no trunk, so the same gather changes its spread by nine.
Asserting conservation would force the animation to draw a nine-fold speed-up. So: where
the skater gathers in the rate must not fall, where they open out it must not rise, and
every spin must contain a wind-up that does both.

**A change of foot is exempt from the shape rule** — a step-over costs speed through the
transfer, not through anything the body opened out, so a skater can come out of it tighter
and slower. The exemption is not a hole: the other side is asserted instead, that a change
of foot may cost rotation or hold it and never make it.

**The checker found two faults in the authoring that produced it, and one in itself.** The
camel's second segment turned faster while the chest dropped further out; and the axis was
taken once from the first centred segment and held, which on a combination measured the
upright's markers from a point 40 cm away and reported a spin gathering in as one opening
out. The axis is read per frame from the blade now.

## Decisions locked

- **A path segment may carry its own radius**, defaulting to the move's. Optional, so
  every older move is byte-identical.
- **A spin's rotation rate is authored per phase**, in revolutions per second, and the
  span is derived from it. `duration` is the sum of the spans.
- **Centred means the blade's lateral offset equals the path radius**, and only the
  lateral half is judged.
- **The final wind-up is not a position**, per the handbook, and carries `windup: true`.
- **A position is reached in a segment that does not claim it** — transitions get their
  own unlabelled segments, which is also how the handbook counts them.
- **A free leg that is up and behind cannot be brought down.** Measured. The camel's leg
  does not move and no combination in this guide contains a camel.
- **Spins open at half playback speed.**
- **Section order on the elements page is a written rank, and an unranked kind is a build
  failure.**
- **Playwright is installed `--no-save`**, and this Mac needs `--include=dev`.
