## Done

- **The arms were not undepthed. They were depthed wrongly.** `viewProfile` drew the legs
  sorted by `NEARER`, then the torso, then both arms in fixed `L, R` order. Arms were last
  unconditionally, so an arm behind the chest drew in front of it and the further arm drew
  over the nearer one whenever L was the far side. Draw order is a depth claim; it was
  being made, and made wrong about half the time by construction.
- **One sorted list, per segment.** Arms split at the elbow, torso split at the waist, legs
  split at the knee, all ordered against one `NEARER` expression. Per-segment is not
  fastidiousness: an upper arm can be behind the chest while the forearm is in front of it,
  and no single ordering of the whole limb can say both.
- **The legs were split because it was measured, not argued.** I claimed a knee never falls
  on the far side of the torso from its own blade and offered to measure instead. Over 2,010
  samples — 201 of each of five moves, both legs — the knee and the **blade** straddle the
  torso's depth in **227**, the knee and the **ankle** in **294**, in every move including
  the spiral. 227 is the load-bearing figure because the old sort ordered a leg at
  `near(q)` and `q` is the blade.
- **Casings are retracted, not suppressed.** Every segment in front of something gets a
  halo; each casing is its own segment's path pulled back from any internal joint by the
  casing's half-width, so it stops at the joint instead of painting over its sibling's
  stroke. The invariant is **casings = segments − 1** and it holds in every panel. The first
  attempt suppressed a casing when its sibling sorted next to it, which — because the two
  halves of a limb are usually contiguous in depth — removed the halo from the second half
  of nearly every limb.
- **The `LH`/`RH` override was silently discarded, and the 12 cm threshold is gone.**
  `moves.js` says "any key may override with `LH` / `RH`"; the loop assigned `k[w+'H']`
  unconditionally for every key of every move, so an authored hand was computed, stored and
  overwritten before anything read it. Nothing failed and no checker looked — the shape of
  the `featured` filter that absorbed the twizzles. The fix is one guard on an `authored`
  flag, and that flag now also decides whether the guide names which hand is which: a move
  whose keys author a hand gets letters, one that does not gets none. No threshold.
- **One asymmetric pose, authored deliberately:** the spiral with the left arm across the
  body and the right reaching back — the pose a coach names when they say a spiral is held
  against a checked shoulder line. From behind, the left forearm crosses the chest and the
  right upper arm passes behind it. `verified: false`, like everything else.
- **The elbow is dropped past the end-on threshold, joint circle included.** `model.md`
  already said stop drawing the elbow when the arm is near end-on; that rule predates the
  joint circle, which had been sitting on the shoulder-to-hand line ever since. The
  threshold is one stroke width of *projected* bend, which is the quantity the rule is
  about — the 3D bend can be large while the picture shows none.
- **Three facts moved from the renderer's head into the DOM:** `data-depth` (the value the
  sort consumed), `data-arm-side` and `data-boot`. Same argument that put `data-limb` on the
  legs. Depth mattered most: it is the one fact the whole change turns on and was the only
  thing the markup threw away.

## The boots — not an arm, and the only thing here that is live on published pages

Martyn found it in the waltz jump's rear view. It is every rear view in the guide.

- **A degenerate basis.** The end-on boot glyph took its roll from the leg's up-axis *after*
  the boot's in-view direction had been projected out of it. That projection is right in the
  side-on branch, where the boot's direction lies in the view plane. In the end-on branch it
  is worse than useless: that branch runs *because* the boot points at the camera, so the
  vector being projected out is near-zero and its direction is noise. It annihilated the
  up-axis's vertical component, `roll` collapsed to ±90°, and the boot was drawn lying on
  the ice. Which way it fell was decided by the sign of the pitch.
- **Scale.** In the rear view a skating boot points along ±t, so `tx = bd[1]` is *exactly*
  zero and `ty` is the sine of the pitch. Swept over 234 end-on glyphs across four moves and
  both views, **140 were past 60° of roll**; after the fix, 32. The extended edge checked
  exhaustively rather than sampled — all 321 frames, 379 end-on glyphs — is **379 of 379**
  past 60°, rolls spanning only −90.0° to −91.7°, against **0 of 379** after, −7.5° to
  −13.0°. Every frame, hard over.
- **And the spiral and teapot showed 0 of 41.** Their skating pitch is authored as *exactly*
  0, which makes the degenerate vector exactly zero and the projection a no-op. Authoring a
  physically realistic half-degree of pitch is what triggers this; authoring nothing hides
  it. So the element the standing rule says to shoot first is the one that cannot show the
  defect — and it has been in `shots/positions/` since those dumps were taken.
- **The edge dot was being drawn on feet in the air.** Found by measuring a claim of mine
  that turned out false. Of 300 skating-boot glyphs, **300 are byte-identical** after the
  derivation fix — so on a boot that is on the ice it moves no pixel. Of 36 free-boot
  glyphs, **none are**, and each differs by exactly one removed circle. A free boot's
  direction is built from the shin, so it is not along the track, which is exactly where
  `facing` and the lateral projection part company. Neither side was right: the dot marks
  the *biting* edge, so on a raised foot it asserts ice contact the model denies, in
  `--free`, which simultaneously says the foot is off the ice. The mark goes.
- **`freefoot.mjs` cannot fail for the reason its comment gives.** The boot-to-shin angle is
  fixed at exactly `ANKLE_FREE` by construction inside `bootDir`, so "the boot holds the
  ankle square" is unfalsifiable. What it measures — boot angle from level — is a property
  of the leg's pose, which its own comment admits. It is a check on the leg wearing the name
  of a check on the foot. And it reads **keyframes only**: the waltz jump's free boot reaches
  **88.4° at f = 0.428** (2000-sample sweep) where its worst keyframe is 55.8° — **28.4°
  past the 60° limit**, in frames the checker never looks at. The same gap `lean.mjs` was
  rewritten per-frame to close, two sessions ago, in this repository.

## `ANKLE_FREE` is not a knob, and that is the finding

Asked to try a value and shoot it. The value is 25° and the shot says the constant is not
the lever. Raising it rotates the boot further toward the shin — which *is* pointing the toe
— but the shin points up on some poses and down on others, so the poses move in opposite
directions. Measured by recovering the free branch's own decomposition from `bootDir`
(identity error 0 at the current value), so no second copy of the model was written:

| `ANKLE_FREE` | spiral | teapot | extended edge | waltz run-out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10° — now | +30.1 | −55.2 | +53.6 | +55.0 |
| 25° — shot | +15.1 | −40.3 | **+68.4** | **+69.9** |
| 40° | +0.1 | −25.4 | **+82.8** | **+84.3** |

Two improve, two get worse; at 40° the four span 0.1° to 84.3°. **There is no value that
helps all four**, because plantarflexion drives the blade wherever the shin already points:
behind you with the foot high it lifts the toe, behind you with the foot low it drives it
further at the ice.

Across every keyframe in the repo the free boot sits **6.8° to 55.8° off level**. The three
held positions read **+30.1** (spiral), **+53.6** (extended edge) and **−55.2** (teapot);
the waltz's +55.0 is its run-out, not a held position.

So the question for a coach is not "what should this number be". It is **does the free foot
in each of these positions look like that**, and `Arms.dc.html` shows the same held position
at both values, cropped to the boot, so the answer can be given by pointing.

## Decisions locked

- **A depth claim is made once, against one expression, at one granularity.** Arms, torso and
  legs all split; one sorted list; `NEARER` per view as the only source of the camera.
- **Casing is retracted from its own joint, never suppressed.** A rule that depended on what
  sorted next to a sibling gave a halo to some segments and not others arbitrarily.
- **Which hand is which follows the data, not a measurement of it.** An `authored` flag, not
  a threshold. Every other constant in this repo comes from the equipment; there is no
  physical fact behind "how far apart must two hands be before we name them".
- **A fact the renderer knows belongs in the DOM.** `data-depth`, `data-arm-side`,
  `data-boot`, after `data-limb` and `data-casing`. A checker that cannot read the claim
  cannot check it.
- **The arms invert the standing rule, and the boots invert it again.** Rig changes are shot
  on the spiral first because the spiral is where a wrong depth sign shows. For the arms it
  is the other way round: a spiral carries them wide, level and apart, so the two arms never
  overlap each other there and the change cannot show, while a jump gathers them to 22 cm and
  rotates 180°. For the boot roll it is the same: the spiral's skating pitch is authored as
  exactly 0, which is the one value that hides the bug. **Shoot the spiral first — and do not
  conclude anything from it alone.**
- **A pose is never edited to satisfy a checker.** Nothing in `moves.js` was changed. The one
  new pose is authored deliberately and marked as such.

## Open questions

- **Is the torso in the sort, or a layer?** The one genuine design decision left, and it
  wants an eye rather than an argument. `Arms.dc.html` prints the difference where it shows:
  on the spiral's held frame the two options differ in 4 of 10 overlapping pairs, on the
  waltz peak 2 of 15, and at some frames not at all — so judge it across a scrub, not on one
  frame. Assertion 1 above already fails the layer option by construction, which is an
  argument but not a picture.
- **Is a free boot really held within 10° of square to the shin?** See the table. Moving it
  moves every free boot in the guide, which is why it is not mine to move.
- **Is `onIce` the right shape?** Unchanged from Session 04 — and the boot work makes it
  cheaper: the dot's screen side is now derived from the boot's own lateral axis projected
  through the view, which is exactly what a rolled boot needs.
- **Are the poses right at all?** Standing since Session 01. `verified: false` throughout.
- **Is 1.8:1 enough weight separation in a rink?** Unchanged.

## A working rule worth adding to the house style

This repository's failures are not arithmetic. They are **descriptions of things that
changed**: a filter naming what it hides, a comment promising an override the loop discards,
a checker reading keyframes for a renderer that interpolates, `NEARER` and the toe/heel test
as two expressions of one fact. Twelve rounds of review on this artefact found that shape
twelve more times, in prose written to argue against it. The defence is not care. It is
declining to state by hand anything the code already knows — and where a document must state
it, printing the measurement next to the claim.
