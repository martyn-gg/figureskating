## Done

### 1 — the boot roll, and what was actually wrong

Session 05's diagnosis was right and the fix goes one step further than it.

The end-on glyph's roll came from an up-axis built **in the view**: project the leg, then
subtract the projected boot direction. Confirmed exactly as described, with the same
numbers, measured through the real renderer rather than a model of it — `tools/frame-svg.mjs`'s
DOM stub, so the code under test is the code that ships:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| end-on glyphs past 60° of roll, all four moves, both views, every frame | **1102 of 1828** | **255 of 1828** |
| of those, on a skating boot | **558** | **0** |
| extended edge, exhaustively | **379 of 379**, −90.0° to −91.7° | **0 of 379**, −7.1° to −10.4° |
| spiral + teapot, skating boots | **0 of 642** | 0 of 642 |

The 255 that remain are all the spiral's free boot, drawn at about 176°, and that is
correct: in a spiral the foot is above the knee, so the boot's opening genuinely faces
down and forward. The roll limit is asserted on skating boots, where it means something a
blade on ice has to obey.

That last row is the whole reason it survived four sessions, and Session 05 called it: a
skating pitch authored as exactly 0 makes the subtracted vector exactly zero and the
subtraction a no-op. **The element the standing rule says to shoot first is the one
element that could not show this.**

**Where it goes further.** Session 05 proposed taking the raw projected leg direction in
the end-on branch. That is right in the sense that it is not degenerate, but it is still a
second expression of a fact the model already owns. `ankleOf` builds the boot's up-axis —
the leg's direction with the boot's own direction taken out, in 3D — and then places the
ankle along it. So there is exactly one honest source: take the boot's direction back off
`ank − blade` and you have `ankleOf`'s axis by construction.

That matters, not just tidiness. The renderer was deriving its axis from the **second-pass
knee**, one iteration on from the first-pass knee the ankle sits on. Measured: **up to
11.4° apart on the waltz jump**, over 1° in 240 of 282 rear-view frames. The boot was
being drawn about an axis the drawn ankle is not on, so the drawn shin entered the drawn
boot off-centre. It is *Two knees, and they are not the same knee* from Session 04, seen
from the other side.

With the axis taken from the ankle, `tools/boot.mjs` asserts the drawn roll against
`ankleOf`'s output and the worst disagreement across all 1828 end-on glyphs is **0.00°**.

### 2 — the edge dot

Removed from every free boot. It marks the *biting* edge, so on a foot in the air it
asserts contact `--free` is simultaneously denying.

Its screen side now comes from the boot's own lateral axis projected through the view,
rather than from where the toe points. Checked exhaustively rather than sampled:

- **1245 of 1245** skating end-on glyphs put the dot in exactly the same place under both
  derivations — byte for byte — because a blade on the ice lies along its own tracing and
  the two routes cannot disagree there.
- **583 of 583** free glyphs lost it, each differing by exactly one removed circle and
  nothing else.

Session 05 measured 300 and 36 of these; this is every frame of every move.

### 3 — `tools/boot.mjs`, the ninth checker

Both assertions from the handoff, both absolute, across every frame of every move in both
profile views — 5132 boot glyphs, 1828 of them end-on.

1. The edge dot is on a skating boot and never on a free one.
2. An end-on boot's drawn roll equals its own up-axis, and a skating boot is never drawn
   past 30° of it — the 28° the cuff allows (`shin.mjs`) plus what the projection does to a
   boot that is also pitched. Measured maximum after the fix: 11.8°.

**The expected roll does not come from the renderer.** It comes from where `ankleOf` put
the ankle, which the leg is drawn to. If the model's up-axis and the renderer's ever part
company, the picture shows a boot whose opening is not where the leg enters it, and this
catches it. That is the `NEARER`-versus-toe/heel pattern again: two expressions of one
fact, made to be one.

No `--break` flag. Reproducing the old derivation would mean a second copy of the
renderer, which this repo does not keep; the pre-fix counts above are in the file's header
instead, dated and measured.

## The third defect, which is a design call and was not fixed

The same shape as the other two, found while measuring them, and **not** in Session 05.

The side-on branch draws the boot with a 2×2 whose two columns are two projected 3D axes.
Where those project onto nearly the same screen line the matrix goes singular and the
glyph collapses to a stroke. That is not a rare frame:

| rear view, free boot, side-on branch | frames | up-axis within 20° of the camera | within 5° |
|---|---|---|---|
| extended edge | 263 | **263** | 0 |
| teapot | 100 | **100** | 69 |
| waltz jump | 322 | 216 | 6 |

The renderer squares the up-axis against the boot's direction *in the view*, which keeps a
legible glyph at the price of drawing a boot that is not the boot in the model. Taking the
squaring out draws the honest thing, which is a boot collapsed to a line — I rendered both
and neither is right.

**What those frames actually hold is a boot seen from above its own opening**, and there
is no glyph for that. `bootSide` and `bootEnd` are the only two, and the branch chooses
between them on how much the boot's *direction* points at the camera, which is the wrong
question in this case. A third glyph, or a rule for declining to draw one, is a design
decision — so the squaring is left in place, commented as a known fudge with the numbers
beside it, and flagged here.

It is measurably almost the drawing that was always there: unchanged on **every** side-on
skating glyph in the repository, and inside 1.3° on all but seventy-five free ones.

## Decisions locked

- **A boot is drawn about the axis its ankle sits on.** One vector, from `ankleOf`, in 3D,
  projected like everything else. Not from the knee — the renderer's knee is one iteration
  past the ankle's, and 11.4° away at worst.
- **A projection is done in 3D and then projected, never approximated in 2D.** The 2D
  subtraction was the bug in both branches; it is only still present in the side-on one
  because removing it needs a glyph that does not exist.
- **The edge dot follows the boot's lateral axis, not the toe.** They agree on every
  skating boot because a blade lies along its tracing, and part company on every free one,
  which is where the wrong dot was.
- **A checker's expected value comes from the model's output, not from the renderer's
  expression.** `boot.mjs` reads `ankleOf`'s ankle. Two expressions of one fact is this
  repository's recurring failure and the defence is to have one.
- **`freefoot.mjs` stays red.** A checker that is telling the truth is not a problem to be
  solved by moving a constant.

## Applied

**1 — one sorted list.** `viewProfile` builds `items` of `{part, depth, paint}` and sorts
once against `NEARER[mode]`. Arms split at the elbow, torso at the waist, legs at the knee.
Casing goes to everything but the furthest item, retracted from its own internal joint by its
own half-width. `data-depth` on every segment, `data-arm-side`, `data-arm`, `data-torso`,
`data-leg`, `data-elbow`, `data-hand`, `data-end-on`.

**2 — the elbow threshold**, `ARM_END_ON = ARM_W`, one stroke width of *projected* bend,
joint circle included, exported so the checker uses the same constant.

**3 — the `LH`/`RH` guard**, in `src/lib/moves.js`, plus the `PH()` helper that marks a hand
`authored`. `armsAuthored(move)` is exported from `body-frame.js` and read **once per move**
at mount, not per frame.

**4 — `spiralCheck`**, the one asymmetric pose. It is derived from `MOVES.spiral` rather than
restated, so the two share their legs and cannot drift; only the hands are authored. It picks
up every checker for free and appears on `/rig` with no element page, exactly as the teapot
did. `verified: false`.

**5 — `tools/arms.mjs`, the tenth checker.** Six assertions, 3,208 frames, 30,991 segments.

## What the fork proposed that was NOT taken

- **`bootDirAt`**, the `ANKLE_FREE` probe. Its own comment says it must not outlive the
  review artefact, and it is a second decomposition of `bootDir`. Left in the fork.
- **`torso: 'layer'`**, the comparison option. The repository ships one answer; see below.
- **The fork's boot roll fix**, which takes the raw projected leg direction. Part one of this
  handoff had already taken it from the ankle instead, which is `ankleOf`'s own axis and
  therefore assertable to 0.00°. The fork's version is right about the diagnosis and one step

- **`bootEnd`'s `latX`** was adopted in substance — part one already derived the dot's side
  from the boot's lateral axis and measured the same 1245-of-1245 agreement — and its richer
  `data-edge-dot` value (`skater-left` / `skater-right`) was taken over the empty attribute.

## The torso is in the sort, not a layer

Session 05 left this as the one genuine design decision, wanting an eye rather than an
argument. It has an answer, and it came out of writing the checker: **assertion 1 fails the
layer option by construction**, because that option does not sort the torso and so its drawn
order cannot be monotonic in the depth it drew. Measured, not asserted: rendering the layer
option and running the checker gives **7,005 order failures**.

That is not a picture, and Session 05 was right that a picture would help. But it is not a
matter of taste either: the layer option makes a depth claim it cannot state, and the repo
has a rule about claims it cannot state.

## Every assertion broken on purpose

| broken | failures |
|---|---|
| `items.sort()` removed | **13,263** order |
| torso drawn as a layer | **7,005** order |
| `back()` returning 0 — casing not retracted | **26,659** casing |
| `ARM_W` raised to 6.0 | **11,123** ink |
| renderer using the 3D bend instead of the projected one | **447** elbow |
| only one hand drawn | **3,208** hands |
| the `LH`/`RH` guard removed from `moves.js` | authored hands vanish entirely |

Raising `ARM_END_ON` alone does **not** fail, and that is correct rather than a gap: the
checker reads the same constant the renderer does, exactly as `boot.mjs` reads `ankleOf`. The
assertion is that the renderer agrees with its own threshold, so the mutation that has to
fail is the one that changes the *quantity* — 3D bend for projected — which is the bug the
rule was written against in the first place.
