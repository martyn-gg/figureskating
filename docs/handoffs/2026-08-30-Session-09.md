# `onIce`, decided — and two of the gaps it was meant to close turned out not to need it

**Session 09 · 30/08/2026** — Cowork. Follows Session 08, which finished the syllabus half.
This session answered the question four handoffs had deferred, and corrected two things
Session 08 got wrong on the way.

**A pose can hold two blades.** Twelve checkers, eleven green, one deliberately red as
before. 244 pages, 6644 internal links, 48 exercises still asserting against the documents.

## The decision, and its shape

`skate` did not become an array. It stayed single-valued and **changed job**: it is now the
**reference blade** — the one `buildPath` builds the path from and the one the hip hangs off
— and `null` still means airborne. A second blade is declared on the foot itself,
`onIce: 'blade'`, carrying its own direction of travel where the two feet oppose.

That shape came from British Ice Skating rather than from convenience. Their slip-step
definition puts the weight over one leg while both blades are down, so a second blade is not
a second skating foot and the model should not pretend it is.

**The second blade's edge is derived and never stored.** Both blades are on one circle, one
circle is one lobe, one lobe is one `lobeSense` — so the second edge letter falls out of the
first blade's state and the second foot's direction of travel. `secondFoot` in `skating.js`
is four lines. Storing it would have been the second source of truth `style.md` bans and
would have made an impossible pair like RFI & LFI representable.

**Their paper agrees, and neither was told.** The Skills 1 slalom writes the pairs out — RFI
& LFO, RFO & LFI, LBI & RBO, LBO & RBI — and all four have equal lobeSense. Same agreement
that already held for mohawks and choctaws.

**Two shapes, and there is no third.** Both feet the same way round gives one outside edge
and one inside — a two-foot power change or an Ina Bauer. One forward and one backward gives
the *same* letter on both, which is a spread eagle, and that is what the turnout is for.

## `lean.mjs` did not have to be weakened

This was the expected cost and it was not paid. The outside of the left foot and the inside
of the right foot are **both the skater's left side**, so two blades bite on one side, the
body leans over that side, and one lean satisfies both routes on both feet. The file now
asserts that *every* blade on the ice leans the same way, which is a stronger claim than it
made yesterday.

The rendered rear view says it better than the argument does: two edge dots, on the same side
of the body, in two different colours.

## `tools/twofoot.mjs`, the twelfth checker

Five assertions, every one broken on purpose:

| broken | failures |
|---|---|
| a second blade declared on an airborne pose | 8 |
| a free foot declared as being on the ice | 39 |
| two blades authored in the same place | 22 |
| the second blade's lobe sense flipped | 321 frames |
| the derivation inverted against BIS's pairs | 4 |

The z assertion is the one `docs/model.md` asked for in as many words: the lunge "would have
passed every checker with the trailing foot lifted 30 cm". A blade claimed on the ice is
within 3 cm of it and a foot not claimed is at least 5 cm clear — the existing poses sit at
0–2 and 10–117, so there is a real gap and nothing had to be nudged to pass.

The fifth is the only assertion in the file with an **independent source**. The other four
check the model against itself; that one reads BIS's own pairs out of the PDF with
`pdftotext` and puts `secondFoot` back against them. It skips with a notice on a clean clone,
the same call `syllabus.mjs` makes.

## `shin.mjs` earned its generalisation on the first run

Five checkers read `skate` as "is this the skating foot" when they meant "is this foot on the
ice". Those were the same question until today. All five now read `bladesDown`.

The first draft of the new pose failed shin lean at 31° and 32° against the boot's 28° — **on
the second blade**, which the old predicate would never have looked at. It would have
shipped.

Fixed by measurement rather than by argument, which is the house rule and it paid again:
sweeping hip height against shin lean shows the angle is almost entirely a function of leg
extension and barely of lateral offset — 29° at hipZ 92, 25 at 94, 21 at 96. So the hip came
up and the feet stayed where they were. Moving the feet would have done nothing.

`freefoot.mjs`'s predicate change was proved to be a no-op the same way: the old and new
outputs diff clean, byte for byte.

## The pose, and why it is the position and not the change

`twoFoot` on `/rig`: RFI & LFO held on one lobe, three keys, both pitches a real half-degree
rather than zero. (An authored zero makes the boot's up-axis subtraction a no-op and hid the
end-on roll collapse for four sessions. A new pose should never be the one that hides the
next one.)

**It is the position either side of a power change, not the change.** A change of edge passes
through a **flat**, where the lobe has no centre, `lobeSense` is zero, and `lean.mjs`'s TRACK
route has nothing to assert against — `sign(n)` must pass through zero and there is no right
answer at the crossing. Authoring the change would have meant granting that checker an
exemption on the first day it held two blades, which is the one cost this session set out not
to pay. Changes of edge are the edge diagram's job here and always have been.

**No element page**, as the teapot had none. BIS names no element called a two-foot edge, and
inventing one to fill a gap is the thing this repository does not do.

## Two gaps were misread, and the definitions document fixed both

Session 08 named the slip chassé and the crossed step behind as `onIce` gaps. Reading BIS's
*Definition of Steps, Turns & Movements* properly says otherwise.

**The slip chassé is not a two-blade element.** *Slip chassé* and *slip step* are two separate
entries. The slip **step** is the one with both blades flat on the ice. The slip **chassé** is
a simple chassé whose free foot slides off the ice in front rather than being lifted — and
Skills 5 exercise 3 asks for the chassé, at steps 5 and 10. Session 08's `notCovered` line
gave the slip step's definition under the slip chassé's name. Corrected. The gap is now the
same class as open versus closed mohawks: free-foot placement, which a blade cannot draw, and
`lfo-chasse.md`'s own prose already said so.

**The crossed step behind is a defined element**, and its definition places the free foot on
the outer edge side exactly as the crossed step in front does. So it draws a crossover's
tracing and is one entry in `TRANSITIONS` away. Not built.

**The push or touch down stays a gap, and now for the right reason.** The rig can hold it;
their definitions document names no such step, so there is nothing to link to. The line said
the rig was the problem. It said so wrongly, and now says what is actually missing.

So the honest tally: **`onIce` closes one syllabus gap outright**, the two-foot power change,
and unblocks the Ina Bauer and the spread eagle, which are not in Skills 1–8 at all. That is
a weaker case than the opening prompt carried, and it was put to Martyn before the work
started rather than after.

## The competition question — logged, not built

Martyn asked mid-session whether anything is missing for national competitions, and asked for
a log rather than a build. `docs/gaps-competition.md`.

Read from BIS's site: the championship entry ladder ties Skills 6 to Basic Novice, Skills 7
to Advanced Novice and Skills 8 to Junior and Senior — **a fact the Skills 8 page should
carry and does not**, and the cheapest useful item in the file. It also names a whole second
ladder, the *National* tests, Technical and Components, which gate entry alongside Skills and
which the guide has never heard of.

Everything else in that file is explicitly flagged as **not read from any document** — a
shopping list for a human with a browser, not content. The headline is that there are 182
elements and not one of them is a spin, and that this is not an oversight: `hipYaw` is
measured from the direction of travel and a spin has none, so spins are a second rig. The
real decision is whether this is a field guide to edges and turns, which it nearly is, or to
figure skating, which is not a weekend.

## Decisions locked

- **`skate` is the reference blade and stays single-valued.** Two blades is a fact about
  feet, not about which foot the skater is on.
- **The second blade's edge is derived from a shared lobeSense**, never authored.
- **The rig holds the two-foot position; the edge diagram draws the change.** No checker was
  given an exemption to make a pose fit.
- **No second tracing in the top-down view.** That view's invariant is that the tracing is
  true and only the body is enlarged. A second line would be four times too far out at body
  scale and invisible at true scale; the two boot glyphs in two edge colours carry the fact.
- **A gap line gets rewritten when it becomes wrong, not deleted.** Three were.

## Open questions

- **Is the prose right?** Unchanged and still the largest thing outstanding. 48 exercise
  paragraphs and 182 element pages, every one `verified: false`.
- **Is the `twoFoot` pose right?** New, and by the same non-expert as everything else. The
  claim most worth a coach's eye is that both blades bite on the same side of the body, which
  is what makes one lean serve two feet.
- **The flat.** Needed by the slip step and by any rig change of edge. BIS define it as the
  *double* tracing of a skate that is straight — two lines, not a third colour, which is worth
  knowing before anyone reaches for a token. `edgeCol` is a bare ternary in five places and
  would silently colour a flat as an inside edge: the featured-filter failure again.
- **The Ina Bauer and the spread eagle** are now possible and unbuilt.
- **The cheap wins, still outstanding:** the triple 3-turn, the 2½ twizzle, the crossed step
  behind, the slip chassé. All four are one more repetition or one more entry in an existing
  table.
- **The competition question**, above. It is the biggest fork in the repository.
- Everything still open from Session 06: the glyph for a boot seen from above its own opening,
  the free-boot angle, re-shooting.

## One workflow note worth keeping

`state-of-play.md` has said since Session 01 that every git command against this folder
leaves a `.lock` behind. That is true of the **bridge VM's** shell and not of the Mac's own.
Running `git status` through the desktop MCP, which executes natively rather than in the
mounted Linux VM, left nothing behind — checked with `find .git -name '*.lock'` immediately
after. That is the same route the build has to take anyway: `node_modules` is built for
macOS, so `npm run build` fails in the VM with a missing rolldown binding and always will.

So the split is narrower than it looked. Claude can edit in the VM and run git and the build
natively; only the VM's git is the trap.
