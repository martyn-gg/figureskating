# Edges and turns, finished — and the fifth assertion that had to exist first

**Session 10 · 30/08/2026** — Cowork. Follows Session 09, which decided `onIce`. This session
answered the bigger fork and then did the first half of the answer.

**206 elements, 268 pages, 7,739 internal links.** Twelve checkers, eleven green, one
deliberately red as before.

## The direction, which is now settled

Martyn's call: **both halves, in that order.** Finish edges and turns first, so there is
something to show people. Then grow into spins, dance and competition-level requirements —
**on their own tab**, so a beginner is not overwhelmed by them.

`docs/gaps-competition.md` stops being a fork and becomes a backlog. Three structural
decisions were added to the end of it, because each costs a line now and a migration later:

- **The tab is a view, not a URL namespace.** An element is written once and appears in as
  many lists as want it — that is what lets one three turn sit in a British, a Canadian and an
  American test without being written three times. Splitting `/elements/` into two trees would
  fork that and break every link into it. A spin still lives at `/elements/<slug>/`; it is
  simply not on the first tab's list.
- **A listing filter names what it shows, not what it hides.** Not hypothetical: the home
  page's featured list was once "everything except the four kinds that existed", and the
  twizzles added later fell through it and landed amongst the jumps. It would have shipped.
- **What decides the split should be data.** There is already a good key and it costs nothing:
  *does this element appear in a test the guide holds?* Derived, self-maintaining, and it
  gives an honest answer to "why is this not on the first tab". A hand-kept list of advanced
  slugs would be wrong within two sessions.

## Four elements, and the sequences that decided their flags

24 pages. Every one of the four is a movement British Ice Skating defines, and **the flags
came from their sequences rather than from their prose definitions**, because the definitions
constrain placement and are silent on the edge.

| element | flags | confirmed against |
|---|---|---|
| triple 3-turn | `['three','three','three']` in `CLUSTERS` | Skills 5 ex 1: LFO and RFO triple 3 turn |
| 2½ twizzle | one entry in `TWIZZLES`, both flags from the count | Skills 7 ex 3, out of a BI counter |
| crossed step behind | the **cross roll's**, not the crossover's | every XB in the syllabus runs BO → BO |
| slip chassé | the chassé's exactly | Skills 5 ex 3: RFO slip chassé → LFI |

The crossed step behind is the one worth reading twice. Their definition puts the free foot on
the outer edge side with the leg crossed behind, exactly as the crossed step in *front* does,
and says nothing about the resulting edge. So the flags had to be read off the sequences —
`(11)RFI bracket (12)XB-LBO (13)XF-RBO (14)XB-LBO` — which run outside edge to outside edge
throughout. That is a cross roll's shape. It draws a cross roll's tracing, which is the same
honest collision the guide already lives with between a crossover and a chassé.

October writes step 13 as `XB-RBO` where the current document writes `XF-RBO`. That is the
sixth altered October exercise Session 08 found, confirmed again from the other side.

**Four gap lines closed**, and Skills 5 exercise 3 has an empty `notCovered` for the first
time. 26 gap lines remain across the 48 exercises, all of them either cluster pairings the
guide has no page for or things genuinely outside the model.

**Skills 6, 7 and 8 now carry the championship entry requirement** — the published minimum for
Basic Novice, Advanced Novice, and Junior and Senior. Each also names the *National* tests as
a second BIS ladder this guide does not cover.

## The fifth assertion, and why it had to exist

**Dropping the 2½ twizzle from Skills 7 exercise 3 passed assertion 4 cleanly.**

That is not a bug; it is the assertion's domain. Assertion 4 works on edge codes, so it only
ever sees an element whose entry or exit the document actually writes down — and a turn
reached *through* another turn sits at a derived edge the paper never names. The 2½'s entry is
a counter's exit. There was nothing to catch, and the element would have been shippable while
missing.

So assertion 5 reads the other thing the documents contain: **their names for the movements**.
Each of BIS's words maps to the model keys that would satisfy it; an exercise must list an
element of that kind or name it in `notCovered`. Broken on purpose both ways: dropping the 2½
twizzle and dropping the crossed step behind each produce one WORD failure.

It is weaker than assertion 4 and the file says so. A dropped *mirror* at a derived edge still
gets past it, and catching that would mean parsing their whole sequence into states — a
different and much larger tool.

Two things it taught on the way in, both recorded in its header:

**Read the sequence, not the page.** The first draft matched the whole exercise block and
reported three exercises that were already right. Skills 1's *learning objectives* offer "3
turn or mohawk" as an entry the skater chooses; Skills 3 exercise 6 lists "introduction to a
double twizzle" as an objective of an exercise that is a 1½. An objective is a statement of
purpose, not a requirement to skate something. The body is now cut to the numbered steps —
each `(n)` marker and everything up to the next — which keeps mid-sequence asides like "then
an optional turn to backwards (3 turn or Mohawk)" and drops the column above them.

**BIS write "3 turn"; this guide writes "three turn"**, because `style.md` says use the rink's
words. So the escape that lets an honestly named gap pass had to accept both spellings, or the
guide's own prose fails its own checker. One line was improved rather than exempted: Skills 1
exercise 3's gap now names the three turn and the mohawk the document offers, which its
neighbour on exercise 2 already did and which is more use to a reader either way.

## `tools/drift.mjs` — the prose check, made permanent

Session 08 ran the three drift scans by hand and wrote that they "have to be run after a
batch". They are now `npm run drift`. It **reports and does not fail**: repetition is not
automatically a fault, and judgement is required, which is why it is not in `npm run check`.

One thing had to be right before it was usable. **The unit is the passage, not the file.** The
derived tier keys its prose on direction and edge and never on the foot, so one passage is
deliberately written into four or eight element files. The first run reported 226 five-grams
and 1,090 seven-grams, nearly all of them the generator quoting itself. Identical bodies are
now collapsed before anything is counted; URLs are stripped, because three pages citing one
source is not three pages sharing a phrase; and summaries are scanned per file with the edge
codes masked, since a summary's whole job is to distinguish and the family skeleton would
otherwise match everything.

It earned its place on this batch, twice:

- **Two summaries came out word for word identical to existing ones.** A crossed step behind
  produces exactly the states a cross roll produces, and a slip chassé exactly those of a
  chassé — and the generated summary was keyed on `join`, which all five step transitions
  share. `style.md` says a summary distinguishes rather than describes; two identical ones in
  a list do neither. The two that collide now say what the **foot** does. The three that do
  not collide were left alone, because rewriting settled pages to fix a problem they do not
  have is how a repository loses its history.
- **Two new paragraph openings joined existing threes.** Rewritten — the new instance, never
  the settled one. Re-run afterwards, as the rule requires, and both are gone.

## Decisions locked

- **Both halves, edges and turns first, the second half on its own tab.**
- **The tab is a view, not a URL namespace**, and what it lists is derived from the tests.
- **A movement's flags come from the governing body's sequences, not its prose definition**,
  when the definition constrains placement and is silent on the edge. That is how the crossed
  step behind ended up with the cross roll's flags rather than the crossover's.
- **An objective is not a requirement.** A checker reading a syllabus reads the numbered
  steps.
- **The drift check's unit is the passage.** A generator that writes one passage to eight
  files is not repeating itself.

## Open questions

- **Is the prose right?** Unchanged, and now larger: 206 element pages and 48 exercise
  paragraphs, every one `verified: false`. This is still the highest-value thing a coach could
  read.
- **The flat.** Needed by the slip *step* — which is not the slip chassé, and is still not in
  the guide — and by any rig change of edge. BIS define it as the *double* tracing of a skate
  that is straight: two lines, not a third colour. `edgeCol` is a bare ternary in five places.
- **The Ina Bauer and the spread eagle** are possible since Session 09 and unbuilt.
- **What "finished" means for edges and turns.** Four gap lines closed; the ones left are
  cluster pairings — a rocker into a mohawk, a counter into a twizzle, seven in all — which is
  a question about how a reader searches rather than about the model. Worth settling before
  calling this half done and starting the second.
- **Spins are a second rig**, and that is now the next big piece rather than a hypothetical.
  `hipYaw` is measured from the direction of travel and a spin has none.
- Everything still open from Session 06: the glyph for a boot seen from above its own opening,
  the free-boot angle, re-shooting.
