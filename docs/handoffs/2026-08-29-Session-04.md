## What the rig turned down, and why it matters

Not "we ran out of time" — three different walls, and two of them are one field apart.

- **One blade, and only one.** `skate` is a single field and the tracing, the edge colour
  and both lean assertions read off it. An **Ina Bauer** and a **spread eagle** have two
  blades on two edges drawing two tracings. So does BIS's own **slip step**, defined in
  *Definition of Steps, Turns & Movements* as a step "with the blades of both skates being
  held flat on the ice" — so the gap is one the syllabus's own vocabulary contains.
- **A boot can pitch but it cannot roll.** A **lunge** is one blade on its edge and the
  trailing boot lying on its side. `bootDir` builds a free boot square to the shin —
  `ANKLE_FREE` is 10°, the boot-holds-the-ankle fact already in the model — so a trailing
  leg reaching back and down to the ice ends with a near-horizontal shin and a boot
  pointing straight at the ice, which `freefoot.mjs` correctly calls a pointe. **The whole
  plausible range was measured, not guessed:** at a lunge's hip height there is no legal
  trailing-foot position. The boot only comes back inside 60° once the foot is about 30 cm
  up, at which point it is an arabesque.
- **A spin is the far side of a boundary the model already states.** `hipYaw` is measured
  *from the direction of travel* and a spin has none. That is BIS's twizzle disqualifier —
  "if the travelling stops, it becomes a Solo Spin" — which `tracing.mjs` already asserts
  as the advance going to zero. Spin positions are a second rig rooted in the skater, not
  missing keyframes.

## Decisions locked

- **A listing filter names what it shows.** Excluding the kinds you can think of is how a
  new kind gets listed in the wrong place, silently, and it already happened once.
- **The jumps' learning order lives in `JUMPS`.** It is a fact about the jumps, not a
  presentation choice, so it belongs where every page can inherit it.
- **The held-position list comes from the syllabus, not from memory.** Two of the three
  positions in the previous handoff are not in Skills 1 to 8 at all, and the one that was
  missing — the extended edge — was already on the BIS gap list.
- **Extension is measured to the ankle.** `bootDir` solves its knee from the blade; the
  renderer draws its knee from the ankle, 15 cm nearer the hip. A leg past full reach to
  the blade still draws bent. The first extended edge passed all eight checkers and drew a
  visibly folded free leg on a position called *extended*.
- **A pose that cannot be drawn honestly is not drawn.** The lunge would have passed every
  checker with the trailing foot lifted 30 cm. That is authoring to satisfy a checker, and
  it would have drawn a boot floating above the ice on the one position whose whole point
  is that the boot is on it.

## Open questions

- **Is `onIce` the right shape?** One field, two values, on a free foot: exempt it from the
  free-boot elevation limit, tell the renderer to draw it rolled or on an edge, and let the
  top-down view draw a second tracing or a scuff. The alternative is making `skate` an
  array, which is truer for the Ina Bauer and wrong for the lunge, where the second contact
  is a boot and not a blade.
- **Is a free foot 18 cm off the ice an extended position?** That is where the geometry put
  it once the leg was straight enough to draw straight. BIS asks for extension and a turned
  out free foot and says nothing about height, which is the spiral's job. A coach's eye.
- **The rear view's edge dot.** The extended edge renders identically to the waltz landing,
  which is the same RBO shape and has been through a design pass, so this is not something
  this session introduced — but the dot marking the biting edge sits on the same screen
  side in both, and the skater faces the camera in one and away in the other. Worth
  settling alongside the arms, since it is the same question: which side of the body a
  thing is on, seen from a stated camera. `tools/frame-svg.mjs` renders it in one command.
- **Are the poses right at all?** Standing since Session 01. `verified: false` throughout.
- **Is 1.8:1 enough weight separation in a rink?** Unchanged.
- **Should the airborne state say which foot is about to take the weight?** Unchanged.

  187 pages built, 4,719 internal links resolved, `/elements/extended-edge/` and
  `/elements/teapot/` both generated. So the blind half of this session came out right,
  and, as with the twizzles, that is luck rather than method: the compensating checks

  `check:speed` and `check:offline` cannot run here at all.

  all three views at three points, and the first version — which passed all eight checkers
  — was visibly wrong in the side view. That is the second time a checker-clean pose has
