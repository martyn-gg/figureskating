## What this session did

- **Measured first: `tools/knee.mjs`, `npm run knee`.** Sweeps four knee-direction rules
  (pelvis, half, hip-limited, foot) against hip height and foot yaw on the T at
  pushOffT's first key and on tStop, through `shin.mjs`'s own expression (now exported as
  `shinLean` and `shinInBoot`; `shin.mjs` runs as a checker only when invoked). Then the
  same shins split into forward-over-the-toe and across-the-boot, then the feet moved along
  their own headings.
- **The finding is not quite the one the brief expected.** The old rule did put the lean
  ACROSS the boot (T: 9° forward, 23° across; T-stop trailing blade: 28° across), but the
  total lean `shin.mjs` limits barely moved with the rule (24° → 20° on the T at 92). The
  rule on its own buys about two centimetres of hip. What it changes is that a lean over
  the toe comes out when the foot moves forward along its blade, and a lean across the boot
  does not. Under the old rule no foot placement got the T-stop below a hip of 92; under the
  new one, 12 cm along the blades reaches 88. Tables in `docs/model.md`, *Where a knee
  points*, and from `npm run knee` (which now measures the deepened poses).
- **`kneeFace(pose, which)` in `rig-math.js`, the one expression for every knee.** It
  replaced `anterior(pose.hipYaw)` at 22 call sites (body-frame.js three, and spin, ankle,
  boot, shin, blade, freefoot, turnout, reach, continuity). The refactor was hashed
  identical over all 86 moves before the rule went in.
- **The rule.** A foot with steel on the ice (blade or skid) gets a knee that follows the
  foot's turn off the pelvis, up to `HIP_OUT` 40° out and `HIP_IN` 20° in; past that the
  knee stops and the remainder is tibial twist, which `turnout.mjs` already bounds. Free
  feet, picks and side-boot contacts keep the pelvis (free: circular, the boot is built from
  the shin; pick and side boot: not measured). `bootDir`'s free-foot ankle hinge still uses
  `anterior(hipYaw)` and is deliberately untouched.
- **Three poses broke under the rule and were re-authored**, each to the nearest pose that
  holds both `shin.mjs` and `turnout.mjs` (found by sweep, not by eye):
  - snowplough: 40° toe-in, hip 90 → **35°, hip 93, feet at ±30 not ±34**, shoulders up 3.
    No hip height held both checkers at 40: the hip that fixes the shin straightens the
    knee out of the twist the toe-in needs.
  - ploughBack: 45° out, hip 90 → **40°, hip 93**, shoulders up 3.
  - flip take-off key (t 0.58, "vaulting off the pick"): hip 96 → **97**. Carries to
    doubleFlip and the four flip combinations.
- **Then the T was deepened.** pushOffT's T key: hip 92 → **86**, shoulders 145 → 139, the
  pushing blade 3 cm further out (R n 14 → 17). The second stroke follows through
  `mirrorMove`. tStop: hip 94 → **88**, shoulders 147 → 141, the gliding blade 12 cm ahead
  of the hip (R t 0 → 12) and the trailing blade 6 cm further out along its own heading
  (L n −14 → −20). Worst shin after: T 26°, T-stop 26°, against 28.
- Comments in `moves.js` (pushOffT, tStop, snowplough, ploughBack) and `docs/model.md`
  updated; the model.md hockey-stop paragraph now says where a knee faces.

## For Martyn to judge

1. **The deeper T-stop sits back**: hip 12 cm behind the gliding blade. A braking skater's
   weight does go behind the feet, but how far is your call. Same open question as the
   Session 22 `balance-review` branch (still unmerged).
2. **The snowplough lost five degrees of toe-in** and stands 3 cm taller. Part of its inward
   shin lean is really the boot tipping onto the inside edge, which `shin.mjs` cannot see
   (it measures against a level boot). If a coach says 40° with knees in is normal, the fix
   is a rolled planted boot, not a different pose.
3. Whether a knee should follow a toe turned IN at all (the 20° `HIP_IN` side of the rule).
   Anatomically yes; that is the side that cost the snowplough.
