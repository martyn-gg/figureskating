## Older test names — an addendum, not a structure change

Martyn asked for a lookup for a skater who remembers passing something the guide has no
name for. His framing: *public, an addendum, and it links to no element*.

`/tests/older-names/`, linked once from the foot of `/tests/` and from nowhere else. Four
schemes in order — the medal tests (Preliminary, Inter-Bronze … Gold Star), the numbered
Levels 1–10 of the July 2015 singles manual, the interim field moves (1, 2, 3, BN, INT, Adv
Nov, JNR, SNR), and Skills 1–8. NISA became British Ice Skating in November 2018.

**Only one conversion is published anywhere**, and it is field moves → Skills, from the
Handbook. Ten rungs into eight: 4 and 5 both give Skills 5, 6 and 7 both give Skills 6, 9
and 10 both give Skills 8, and **nobody is ever awarded Skills 4 by conversion**.

**The medal tests convert to nothing.** Searched properly; there is no published mapping and
two renamings sit in between. The page says so rather than guessing, which is the same call
as `docs/style.md`'s ban on cross-body equivalence tables.

**The one carve-out.** The page carries BIS's conversion table, which `style.md` said would
never happen. Martyn's call: it is public, administrative, historical, and there is no
honest prose form of a lookup. Attribution is what makes it acceptable, so it sits in the
table's own `<caption>` — attached to the thing it attributes, where a redesign cannot
separate them — and `style.md` now records the exception so a later session does not delete
the table on sight.

Two things to add later, Martyn's list: the years each scheme was in force, and the elements
each older level asked for, so a medal-test skater can find a *relative* equivalent. That
second one is the comparison style.md already endorses — compare elements, never levels.

## Skills 2 to 7 — forty exercises

Written level by level with `node tools/syllabus.mjs` after each. Three things needed
judgement rather than transcription.

**Clusters the guide has no page for.** From Skills 5 up, BIS writes almost everything as
pairs and triples. Those are listed as **the elements they are made of**, with a
`notCovered` line saying the pairing has no page — rocker-into-mohawk, double-3-turn-into-
mohawk, counter-into-twizzle, 3-turn + change of edge + choctaw. `choctaw-three-rocker` is
the one three-turn cluster that does have a page, and Skills 7 exercise 7 uses it.

**Three real gaps, named where they occur.** The **triple 3-turn** (S5 ex 1) — the guide has
singles and doubles and nothing at three, so that side is listed by the edges it runs on.
The **slip chassé** (S5 ex 3) — BIS's own step with both blades flat, which is the `onIce`
gap again. The **2½ twizzle** (S7 ex 3) — twizzles are keyed by rotation count because the
count decides the exit, and there is no element at that count.

**The probable BIS error is on the page.** October Skills 6 exercise 6 writes the second
cluster's intermediate edge as RBO; a bracket keeps the foot, so from LFI it can only be
LBO, and Skills 7 builds the same cluster and mirrors it correctly. The exercise page says
so in a sentence, phrased as a transposition rather than an accusation.

## Skills 8 — sections, not exercises

**Martyn's call: three entries, one per section**, with October's 2A and 2B held inside
Section 2 rather than given pages of their own. They are two published versions of the same
section, identical until the double twizzle — which is the same device as the "optional
direction" already written inside Sections 1 and 3. `changesInOctober` carries the
difference, the element list covers both routes, and `order` stays 1..3.

Two things had to change to hold it.

**`unit` on the exercises collection** — `exercise` (default) or `section`. The pages printed
the word "exercise", which was true for seven tests and would have rendered "exercise 1 —
Section 1" on the eighth. Where an entry's name already carries the word, the name is the
label and the chip is dropped, so a Skills 8 page reads *BIS Skills 8 · Section 1* rather
than stuttering. Skate Canada and USFS will want their own word again.

**`sections()` in `tools/syllabus.mjs`.** Skills 8 heads its sections `SKILLS 8 – SECTION n`
rather than `EXERCISE n`, and the October 2A page comes out of `pdftotext` overprinted on
itself as `SKILLS88--SECTION SKILLS SECTION22A`. Headings are now recognised by SECTION
followed by a digit and keyed `SECTION n` with the A/B dropped, and **bodies under a repeated
key are appended rather than overwritten** — so Section 2's body is 2A and 2B together and
assertion 4 sees every edge either version names.

Skills 8 is the most gap-heavy page in the guide, honestly so: the **pivot movement** (§1),
the **push or touch down** before the double twizzle, and the **steps crossed behind** (§2,
and they appear at no other level) are all outside the model.

## A sixth altered October exercise

Session 07 found four existing exercises altered without mention in BIS's announcement.
Reading Skills 8 adds one more: **2A and 2B write the middle of three crossed steps as
crossed behind, where the current document writes it crossed in front** between two that are
not. Recorded in `changesInOctober` on Section 2.

## Prose drift — the check that has to be run after a batch

Forty-eight paragraphs written in one sitting grow house tics that are invisible while
writing and obvious in a list. Martyn asked for it fixed properly, and the first pass caught
about a third of it, because it only scanned the paragraph bodies. Three scans are needed:

1. **n-grams over the bodies** — 5-grams in 3+ files, 7-grams in 2+.
2. **n-grams over the `summary:` lines alone.** The worst offenders, and the body scan never
   sees them: **nine of forty-five summaries used "run straight into"**, and the two bracket
   exercises had summaries identical but for one word — in a list, where a summary's whole
   job is to distinguish.
3. **The first three words of every paragraph.** "The second side" opened six of them.

Rewrite the weaker instance, never the accurate one, and **re-run after every pass**: fixing
tics introduces new ones, and "rotates with the lobe" reached three files that way.

What survives is deliberate: technical phrasing that repeats because the exercises repeat,
the "the announcement does not mention it" line in every `changesInOctober`, four October
entries opening "New on 01/10/2026", and Skills 2's intro answering Skills 1's.

One mechanical trap: rewrapping edited paragraphs with `textwrap` eats the blank line after
the frontmatter's closing `---`. It went from fourteen files and was put back.

## Decisions locked

- **An addendum links to nothing.** `/tests/older-names/` carries no element links, because
  the older schemes' requirements are not in the guide and a link would imply they were.
- **One reproduced table, attributed in its own caption**, and `style.md` says why. It is the
  only exception; anything with substance in it is still written from scratch or not at all.
- **Skills 8 is three sections**, with 2A/2B inside Section 2.
- **The governing body's word for the unit is data**, not a hard-coded string in a template.
- **A checker nobody has seen fail is not evidence.** Every level was broken on purpose after
  writing: EDGE on Skills 1, 2, 7 and 8, GEN on Skills 6 and 8.

## Open questions

- **Is the prose right?** Forty-eight paragraphs by a non-expert, every one
  `verified: false`. This is now the whole of what is left on the syllabus half, and it
  cannot be done from here.
- **`onIce`.** The gap list is no longer abstract: two-foot power changes (S1), the slip
  chassé (S5), the push or touch down and the steps crossed behind (S8), plus the Ina Bauer
  and the spread eagle. The syllabus half has now said exactly what a second blade would buy.
- **The triple 3-turn and the 2½ twizzle** — both are one more repetition of something the
  model already holds. Cheap, if the derived tier can count.
- **Clusters as elements.** Seven pairings are currently listed as their parts. Whether they
  deserve pages is a question about how a reader searches, not about the model.
- The older-names follow-ups above; and everything still open from Session 06 — the glyph for
  a boot seen from above its own opening, the free-boot angle, re-shooting.
