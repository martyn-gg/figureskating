## `skate` names the blade the tracing is built from

Read off the call sites rather than off the name, `skate` was doing three jobs:

- **the rig's spatial root** — `body-frame.js` places every drawn point at `p + (q − sk)`,
  so the reference foot is pinned to the path and the skater hangs off it;
- **the blade the tracing is built from** — `edge` and `dir` attach to it, `secondFoot`
  derives the other foot from it, the ice mark is drawn wherever it goes;
- **a claim that the foot is touching** — `onIceOf` returning `'blade'` for it whatever the
  foot said and whatever its height.

The first two are one job and it is **continuous by construction**: the picture is measured
from the reference, so it exists at every frame or the skater has nowhere to hang. The third
is not continuous at all. **A name that has to be continuous cannot mean a thing that is
not**, which is why the decision could only go one way. Reading `skate` as *the blade that is
touching* would make it change in the middle of a span, and at that instant the hip has
nothing to hang from and `edge` and `dir` have no blade to attach to.

It also finishes a sentence started on 19/09/2026. `onIceOf`'s own comment says the contact
*"is declared on the foot for every other foot in this model; now it is for this one as
well"* — which let the reference blade say **which** contact it had and not that it had
**none**, because the fallback was unconditional. **A default that cannot be overridden is
not a default.**

## The second half, which was not about `skate` at all

`lpP` carries `onIce` from the left key alongside `edge` and `dir`, under a comment calling
all three states rather than quantities. It is right about `edge` and `dir`: they are labels
that stay true while the foot moves. **`onIce` is not that kind of thing.** It is a geometric
claim — this foot is within `ON_ICE` of the ice — and a span is the pose interpolating away
from it. A claim the motion falsifies cannot be carried through the motion.

> At the instant of a key, the key's declaration is the truth. **Between two keys, a contact
> holds only if both ends declare it.**

Which is `arrivalOf`, written the day before to *describe* a state and now *deciding* one.

## Three spans in the whole guide, and the pattern in them is the diagnosis

| move | span | what happens | reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| waltz | 0.30 → 0.32 | L departs, z 2 → 30 | L → air |
| waltz | 0.42 → 0.44 | R arrives, z 26 → 1 | air → R |
| changeFootSpin | 0.36 → 0.50 | L departs z 0 → 16, R arrives z 16 → 0 | L → R |

**In all three the departing foot is the outgoing reference blade.** That is the whole reason
half of a symmetrical piece of work was inert: an arriving foot is not the reference at the
key it starts from, so `onIceOf` found nothing on it and the arrival blend fired; a departing
foot **is**, so `onIceOf` answered `'blade'` for the whole span and `bootDir` returned from
the planted branch before the free one was reached.

And the waltz's authoring says it out loud: the key named *"Blade leaves the ice"* is the one
at 0.32, with the blade thirty centimetres above it. The blade left at 0.30.

## The build: one predicate, one interpolation, no data change

`onIceOf` reads the foot's own declaration first — **including a declared absence** — and
falls back to the reference default only where the foot says nothing. `poseAt` writes
`onIce: null` onto a foot that `arrivalOf` reports arriving or departing, for `u > 0`.
`u > 0` and not `u >= 0` because a blade leaves the ice at a moment, and the last key that
declares the contact is that moment.

**No authored data moved.** Nothing in `moves.js` writes `onIce: null`, so every keyframe
answers exactly as it did and every checker reading `move.keys` is untouched by construction.
Everything reading `poseAt` got the honest answer without being changed. That is the roll's
central claim cashed for the third time.

Deleting the `|| 'blade'` fallback and writing `onIce: 'blade'` into the 85 keys that rely on
it was considered and rejected: at a key the fallback is a derivation, not a guess, and
`twofoot`'s assertion 1 is what holds it honest. **The fallback was never wrong at a key. It
was wrong at a frame, because `skate` carries and the contact it implies expires.**

Third change: `stateOf` in `body-frame.js` asked `!po.skate` and called that airborne, which
reads *"is there a reference"* and was used to mean *"is anything on the ice"* — the same
conflation one layer up. The mark is drawn where the reference blade has a contact now.

**Eleven of 441 hashed frames moved**, all in the two moves with a departure. Five of the
eleven are the waltz's top view at every sampled time after the takeoff, because the mark is
cumulative and ending it six frames earlier changes every later frame of that view.

## `twofoot.mjs`'s assertions 1 and 2, per frame, together

They were per keyframe for the same reason and settling one would have opened the same
argument twice: a foot being put down HAS to cross the 5 cm band, so the clearance bound
could not go per frame until the model could say which feet were changing hands — and a foot
changing hands was exactly what assertion 1 had been calling on the ice.

Per frame they are three claims:

1. a foot claimed on the ice is within `ON_ICE`;
2. a foot claimed free is at least `CLEAR`, **unless** it is changing hands — and above
   `CLEAR` that exception does not bite, so a foot dropped thirty centimetres in one span is
   excused by nothing;
3. **a reference blade that is off the ice is one that is changing hands.**

Mutation counts: air 8, float 91, apart 85, sense 963, bis 4, **carry 39**, dip 3192, stray
13294. `carry` is the one to read — it restores the pre-20/09 reading exactly rather than
inventing a mutation, and thirty-nine is the six waltz frames plus the thirty-three of the
change of foot.

**The monotonic descent the specification asked for is deliberately not asserted.** `lpP`
interpolates `z` linearly between the bounding keys, so a dip is arithmetically impossible,
and *reaches its contact* is assertion 2 holding at the contact key, which it already does.
Both would have been second expressions of facts the model already carries.

## The fourth fault, measured — and then fixed

**The rig's root jumped at every reference change, and no checker saw it.** Every drawn point
is `p + (q − sk)`, so changing which foot `sk` is moved the whole skater by the distance
between the two blades, in one frame: **12.54 cm** on the waltz and **14.49** on the change of
foot, against medians of 1.78 and nought. It showed in the **top view only** — the side and
rear are drawn from the hip and cannot see the skater travel across the ice — at 1.4% to 3.0%
of the view against a local rate of 0.3% to 0.6% and `continuity.mjs`'s bound of 6%.

Found by reconstructing `body-frame.js`'s hip placement per frame while checking whether the
contact work above would disturb it. It had been there since the rig was built.

### The path was doing three jobs

`buildPath` returns one curve and three things read it: the **tracing** drawn on the ice, where
each segment carries the `foot` that cut it; the **rig's root**, the reference blade being
pinned to the curve; and the **clock**. While one blade is down for a whole move these are one
curve. `buildPath` chains segments tangent-continuously — its own comment says so, about the
**radius** changing, which is the case it was written for — and a segment's `foot` can change
at the same boundary. Chaining the position there claims the two blades were in the same place.

**And the root had never been given the condition the tracing has.** `skate` names the blade
the tracing is built from and the mark is drawn only where that blade has a contact; the hip
was hung off it whether it was touching or thirty centimetres up. So the waltz's hip followed
the take-off leg backwards, and the change of foot's hip was carried round the spin's own coil
for the forty-six frames when neither blade was down, instead of staying on the spot a spin is
defined by. The obvious repair — displace the path at each reference change — was tried and
measured: it takes the waltz to 5.31 cm and the change of foot to 2.90, and the leftover is
the departing foot's own motion being read as the body's.

### Hold, then displace

> While the reference blade has a contact, the hip is placed from it. **While nothing is on
> the ice the offset is HELD at its last value** — the body carries on as it was. When a blade
> takes the ice, the path is displaced so the hip does not move.

**Holding is what makes one rule cover a jump and a spin, and neither was designed for.** A
flight segment is a line, so a constant offset carries the hip straight through the air. A
spin's coil is a circle whose radius **equals** the centred blade's lateral offset — that is
`spin.mjs`'s own definition of centred — so a constant offset holds the hip on the centre point
for the whole step-over. The spin's answer falls out of a fact the checkers already assert
rather than being arranged.

It also halves the seams: a blade **leaving** the ice is no longer a change of anchor at all,
because the offset it leaves behind is the offset that is held. Only an arrival displaces.

**The path point carries the offset and the renderer reads it from there.** It used to be
re-derived in `body-frame.js` from `pose.skate`, in two places, which was both a second
expression of a fact the path has to know and the thing that gave the root no condition. A
held offset is a function of the history and one pose has not got one. `pose.skate` places
nothing now: it names the blade the tracing is drawn from, which is all it ever meant.

Keyed on the **pose's** contact, never on the segment's `foot`. Those agree on the clock and
not always on the sample — the sample count is rounded per segment, so the change of foot's
path boundary falls at index 162 while the pose's reference changes at 161.

is re-keyed on the anchor now, and its prose says what it measures rather than what it used to.

## What the change of foot looks like now, and what it is actually missing

The mark has a **thirty-three-frame gap** through the transfer, at the tightest part of the
coil, and now two offset coils either side of it. Looked at, it reads as a step-over. It is the
honest picture: the authoring has the left blade down at 0.36 and the right at 0.50 and says
nothing about what is touching in between.

**This handoff first said `twofoot.mjs`'s 5 cm floor on two blades down was in the way. That
was wrong and the measurement says so:** the closest two blades ever come in this guide is
**13.4 cm**, on `twoFoot`, so the floor has never been near a real pose and is not the
obstacle. The obstacle is that the step-over is authored with **no moment when both feet are
down at all** — the left rises from z 0 while the right descends to it, they cross at a
horizontal gap of 1.6 cm with the left 11.7 cm in the air, and there is no instant where both
are on the ice. Authoring a transfer key means re-authoring the step-over so the weight passes
through two feet, which is a question about how a change of foot is actually skated and wants
a coach, not a floor.

## Decisions locked

- **`skate` names the blade the tracing is built from.** Reference, not contact. The rig is
  rooted on it and the tracing drawn from it, both continuous by construction.
- **A contact is declared on the foot, for every foot including the reference one**, and a
  declared absence is expressible. The fallback is what the reference means when it says
  nothing.
- **A contact holds across a span only where both keys declare it.** `onIce` is a geometric
  claim, not a label, so it does not carry the way `edge` and `dir` do.
- **The ice mark is drawn where the reference blade has a contact**, not merely where there
  is a reference.
- **A checker's mutation that has stopped moving its case is not testing it** — `--break=deeper`.
- **The hip is continuous and the tracing is not.** The skater is one body and does not
  teleport; two blades are two blades and their tracings are two curves.
- **While nothing is on the ice the hip's offset is held**, because there is nothing cutting
  the ice to measure from and the body carries on as it was. One rule covers a jump and a spin.
- **The path point carries the hip's offset.** The renderer no longer derives it, and
  `pose.skate` places nothing.
