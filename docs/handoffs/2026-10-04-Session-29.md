## What this session did

- **`tools/frame-hash.mjs`** (new). One hash per move over every frame of all three views,
  through the real renderers. 60 moves in about 10 s on the Mac. Replaces the seven-sample
  `frame-svg.mjs` + `shasum` routine in the workflow note. Run before and after; `diff`
  names the moves that moved. Baseline taken before any code changed and checked
  deterministic.
- **The pin** (`rig-math.js`, *a contact pinned to the ice*). A foot key may say
  `pin: true` (`PIN(t,n,z,pitch)` in moves.js). Over a run of consecutive pinned keys the
  foot is fixed in the world: the world point is taken once at the run's first key from the
  path (point, heading, hip offset) and the authored t, n; every time inside the run gets
  its t, n back from that point. `pinRuns`, `pinnedAt`, `PIN_AGREE` (1 cm) are exported.
  A pin on the reference blade throws.
- **One departure from the spec, deliberately.** The spec put the pass after `poseAt` "in
  the one place that turns a pose into world coordinates". There is no one place: each view
  does it, and eleven checkers call `poseAt` directly. So the old `poseAt` is now
  `poseFree` (still a function of the clock alone, and what `buildPath` reads) and `poseAt`
  wraps it with the pin. A move with no pin gets `poseFree`'s own object back.
- **Top view.** It draws the body at `BS` = 1.2 times life against a true-scale tracing, so
  a physically pinned foot would have been drawn sliding 0.2 of the hip's travel. A pinned
  foot is drawn where it was drawn on its run's first frame (`pinDrawn` in `viewTop`).
- **Reaching for the pick** (spec part B). `arrivalOf` carries `on: 'pick'`, the target
  pitch, a time weight `w` (smoothstep over `PICK_REACH` = 0.25 s) and a span weight `c`.
  `bootDir` blends the free direction to the pick's by `w`; `contactAlongOf` moves the
  authored point from the blade's middle to the teeth by `c`. Both are needed: the first
  look at the frames had the toe 6 cm under the ice at 0.38 and 0.66, because direction
  arrives in the last quarter second and the point cannot. Blade arrivals are untouched.
- **`toePick` is a movement**: glide (hip 62), sink and reach (hip 54), pick in at 0.42,
  ride past it to 0.62 (the pick goes from 56 cm behind the hip to 27, the hip travels
  33 cm), release, rise. The end key's t, n are what the pin computes.

## Then: Martyn's corrections to the stroking change (NOT built yet, first job next session)

The T was drawn as THE way to stroke. Martyn, 04/10/2026:

- **The T is a second-level option, not the only one.** Beginners push out to the side with
  the feet opening like a book (heels together, the pushing foot turned out and driving out
  sideways). Improving skaters then move to the T: a more elegant position, more power, and
  it uses edges rather than a straight line.
- **T stroking is not on one curve.** Each stroke puts a slight curve on its edge, then the
  next stroke on the other foot curves the other way. So a T-stroking tracing is a series of
  shallow alternating arcs, one per foot.

So next session:
1. Forward stroking page: the side push first (what beginners learn), then the T as the
   progression (elegance, power, edges). Keep under 160 words (`house.mjs`).
2. Rigs: `pushOff` back to a side push for the beginner version (a movement: knees bent, push

   as a second rig, e.g. `pushOffT`, ideally drawing two strokes so the tracing shows the
   alternating curves (path: LFO arc then RFO arc of opposite sense, reference blade handing
