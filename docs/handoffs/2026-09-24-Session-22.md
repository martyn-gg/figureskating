## The axis nobody asserts

`lean.mjs` judges the **lateral** half of balance and nothing else. The fore-aft half is asserted
nowhere, and `spin.mjs` says so in as many words: *"whether that balances is a question about
mass. This rig has markers and no mass. That distance is printed, not asserted."* It had been
printed and not read since the spins went in.

## Swept in the body's own frame

Track coordinates mean nothing for a spinning body, so the sweep projects each contact onto the
skater's own anterior axis. The support base runs from the rearmost blade's heel to the foremost
blade's toe, taking each blade's own heading into account.

| move | frames outside | at the hip | at a crude centre of mass |
|---|---|---|---|
| sitSpin | 281 | 27.0 cm behind | 9.1 |
| combinationSpin | 216 | 27.0 | 9.0 |
| teapot | 259 | 17.0 | 4.1 |
| **slipStep** | 285 | **12.0 — the only two-foot one** | 4.0 |
| waltz | 122 | 7.8 | 9.7 |
| extendedEdge | 321 | 3.9 | 2.3 |
| the other fifteen | 0 | — | — |

**Every failure is behind. Not one is ahead.** Six moves, every one a squat, a deep knee or a
stretched free leg. The slip step is the only two-foot move in the list, which makes it the only
case where static balance is beyond argument — the other seven two-foot moves pass.

**It is not a sign convention.** Shoulders and contacting feet both have a median `t` of exactly
0 against the hip across all 105 keys. It is pose by pose.

**The centroid** uses rounded cadaver segment fractions — pelvis .28 at the hip, thorax and head
.30 at the shoulders, each thigh .10, each shank and boot .06, each arm .05 — and they are **not
verified against anything**. Under it the counterweight does its job and the numbers fall, but
nothing changes side, and the worst becomes the waltz's plain *"Set-up on the forward outside
edge"* at 9.7 cm.

## The first fix was wrong, and `shin.mjs` said so

Moving the skating foot back under the body took all four inside the base — and turned
`shin.mjs` red on **exactly the four keys touched**, with its own header as the diagnosis: *"If a
pose needs more than about 28° of shin lean, the foot is in the wrong place under the hip."*

Sweeping both axes showed why. **Shin lean rises about 1.2° for every centimetre the blade comes
back, and the shoulders do not affect it at all.** There is no foot position that satisfies both
constraints. The fix had to be **the blade back and the chest forward** together, and a grid
search over both found the smallest pair per key that holds the centre of mass 1 cm inside and
the shin at or under 28°.

On the teapot the blade ends up **1 cm** from where it started. Its fault was the torso, not the
foot.

**A near miss on the way, worth keeping.** The probe first re-derived `shin.mjs`'s measurement
from the blade and world up, and read **12°** where `shin.mjs` read **31°**. The real measurement
runs the shin from the *ankle* to a knee re-solved from the ankle, against the *boot's* up-axis —
the ankle-behind-the-boot lesson from 30/08/2026, one file along. **Lift a checker's expression;
do not re-derive it.**

## The hip cannot be put over the blade at all

| key | hip is out by | needs the blade back | shin that demands | boot allows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| slip step, held | 7.0 cm | 8 cm | **36°** | 28° |
| teapot, sinking | 16.0 cm | 17 cm | **50°** | 28° |
| teapot, held low | 16.0 cm | 17 cm | **61°** | 28° |

In a deep bend the hip sitting behind the blade is **forced by the boot**, not an authoring slip.
So if this axis is ever asserted, it has to be asserted at a centre of mass, with the free leg and
chest holding the position up.

## For the review

The next session reviews the project rather than continuing it. What follows is what a reviewer
arriving cold should know, and where this session would look hardest.

### What it is

A public field guide to figure skating at **figureskating.guide**: element pages, the British
Ice Skating Skills tests, off-ice training, and two kinds of animated drawing — an edge diagram of
the tracing, and a **body-frame rig** that draws a skater in three views. Astro 7, static, offline
by service worker. Martyn is a skater; the guide is his, and he is the only person in the loop
who can say whether a position is right.

### How it is organised

- **The model** — `src/lib/rig-math.js` (geometry: contacts, the boot, the path, poses),
  `src/lib/moves.js` (21 authored rigs as keyframes), `src/lib/skating.js` (edges, turns and the
  derivation of every element from three flags). About 3,400 lines across the rig's three files.
- **The renderer** — `src/lib/body-frame.js`. It is meant to decide nothing: it reads the model.
- **Twenty-four checkers in `npm run check`** and four out of band that need Playwright, among
  37 tools. Every one was written by breaking the thing on purpose first, with the mutation
  counts in its header.
- **`docs/model.md`** — the design record, 2,322 lines. Every model change since August was
  *specified there before it was built and marked up afterwards* with what the specification got
  wrong. **`docs/state-of-play.md`**, 1,444 lines. **Twenty handoffs**, 5,236 lines.

### The house rules a reviewer will meet

Specify before building. Assert rather than eyeball — and also render and look, because every
visual fault on record passed every checker. Per frame, not per keyframe. One expression per
fact. A checker that has never failed is a decoration, and so is a mutation that cannot reach
its bound. A pose that cannot be drawn honestly is not drawn. **Ask the coach before moving the
pose.**

### Where this session would look hardest

Candidly, and in no particular order.

- **Most checkers check the model against itself.** `twofoot.mjs` says it outright — of its five
  assertions, *"the other four check the model against itself"*; only one has an independent
  source. `spin.mjs` has the ISU handbook and `syllabus.mjs` has BIS. Much of the rest asserts
  that the model agrees with the model. That is valuable and it is not the same as being right,
  and today is the proof: a skater falling backwards passed twenty-four checkers.
- **The constants.** Of the numbers the repository itself lists as wanting a coach —
  `ANKLE_POINT`, `ANKLE_MAX`, `MAX_BLADE_PITCH`, `CLEAR`, the 60° free-boot limit, `HIP_OUT`,
  `HIP_IN`, `SKID_MIN_YAW`, `RAMP` — **only `RAMP` is verified**, answered by Martyn on
  20/09/2026. Today adds a candidate tenth: the segment masses.
- **The content is entirely unverified.** 0 of 284 elements carry `verified.checked: true`; every
  page shows the amber banner. The last several sessions have gone into the rig. The prose, the
  syllabus mapping and the basics' provenance have had far less attention, and they are most of
  what a reader actually reads.
- **The record may have outgrown its reader.** `model.md` at 2,322 lines and handoffs of 500 were
  written so that nothing is lost between sessions, and nothing has been. Whether a person — or a
  model with a fresh context — can still find the decision they need in them is a fair question.
- **The tooling tax.** Half the checkers cannot run in the bridge VM; Playwright is installed
  `--no-save` and vanishes on `npm ci`; long commands time out at 60 seconds and must be
  backgrounded; `git status` through the bridge leaves a lock. Every session pays this before it
  starts. It may be worth one session to remove rather than another to work around.
- **The rig's ambitions against its purpose.** Twenty-one rigs, twenty dated sections in
  `model.md` since mid-August, and a skater who sat down in a position the guide has shipped
  since 20/09/2026. The
  question a reviewer might ask is not whether each piece was done well — the record says they
  were — but whether the rig is the part of the guide that most needed the effort.
