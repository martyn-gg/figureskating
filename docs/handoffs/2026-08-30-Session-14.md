## A spin is an arc of very small radius

`docs/model.md` had spins outside the model: `hipYaw` is measured from a direction of
travel, and a spin has not got one. **Both halves of that are wrong.** A spin's blade has
a direction of travel at every instant — it is going round a small circle — and the
tangent rotates at the rate the body does, so `hipYaw` through a spin is *constant*, not
undefined. What a spin has not got is net displacement, which the model already knew about
from the other side: BIS's twizzle disqualifier, "if the travelling stops it becomes a Solo
Spin", is the advance going to zero, asserted in `tracing.mjs` since Session 03.

**Three spins now hold every assertion in the repository**, at radius 12 cm over three
revolutions, with no new fields and no new machinery: `uprightSpin`, `sitSpin`,
`camelSpin`, all LBI, all anticlockwise — which `lobeSense(L,I,B) = +1` picks without being
told.

**The sit spin is the teapot, spun.** Same fold, same free leg forward, numbers give or
take a sign. **The camel is NOT the spiral**, which is what I assumed and what the ISU
refuted — see below.

**The path's centre of curvature is the spin axis, and it is not free.** A circle's centre
is square to its tangent, so the axis sits at the blade's own `t`, offset laterally by
exactly `radius`. Placing the body relative to the blade places it relative to the axis,
and each marker's distance from it is the circle that marker sweeps per revolution: hip 0 /
shoulders 0 / free foot 17 cm on the upright, 34 / 18 / 46 on the sit, 0 / 48 / 96 on the
camel. Everywhere else in this rig the radius is the lobe and the lateral offset is lean,
two free numbers; in a spin they jointly place the axis.

The sit's hip a third of a metre off the axis is **forced, not chosen**. `shin.mjs` will
not allow a deep sit with the blade under the hip — 84° of shin lean against the boot's 28
— so the foot must travel forward as the hip drops. Same fact that makes the teapot work.

**Where the model stops:** it can say where the axis is and cannot say whether that is
where the skater balances, because balance is mass and this rig has markers. `spin.mjs`
prints the orbits and asserts nothing about them, on purpose.

## The ISU handbook defines the positions as geometry

Martyn downloaded `sources/isu/` and `sources/bis/` mid-session — 14 and 40 documents.
`sources/isu/MANIFEST.md` is new and says what each is and which have been read.

The Technical Panel Handbook, Single Skating 2026-27 defines the three basic positions in
terms the rig can measure directly, which is exactly the outside expectation `new-checker`
demands: **camel** = free leg backwards with the knee higher than the hip; **sit** = the
upper part of the skating leg at least parallel to the ice; **upright** = skating leg
extended or slightly bent and not a camel. Plus: fewer than three rotations is a skating
movement and not a spin, and a position must hold **two revolutions without interruption**.

`tools/spin.mjs` is the seventeenth checker — eighteenth counting `check:prose`. Which
moves are spins is **derived** (one arc, no straights, at least a full turn); which
position a spin claims is **declared**, because that is the claim being tested.

**Its first bug was taking the held interval from the keyframes.** The last keyframe sits
at t = 1.00, so `from` was 1.0 on every move and it sampled a single instant 321 times — a
checker that cannot watch a position being lost. The interval comes from the handbook too:
two revolutions of three is the last two thirds of the clock. Fixing it immediately failed
the sit and the camel, both of which were reaching their position at the very end. Both
re-authored so the position is complete a third of the way in, which is the extended
edge's convention already.

Broken on purpose, six ways: camel's free foot 10 cm lower **102 of 321 held frames** ·
sit's hip raised to 60 cm **197** · upright authored as a camel **321** · camel's free foot
forward instead of back **321** · sweep 1080 → 720 **1 move** · position claim removed **1
move**. The fourth is why "backwards" is tested and not only the knee height: without that
clause the mutation passed, and a leg held forward and high is not a camel.

## `ANKLE_FREE` was an identity, not a limit

**The camel would not draw.** Swept 303,072 free-foot positions: legal ISU camels existed
only with the free foot 35–45 cm above the hip, which is a Y-spin. Where a camel actually
lives, every position that got the knee above the hip drove the free boot past 60° and
`freefoot.mjs` called it a pointe. Same wall the lunge hit.

**Martyn's call, and it was right: go and measure the boot.** Manufacturers publish
stiffness ratings and not angles — Edea 40–95, Jackson 2–95, scales not comparable to each
other. The number is in the injury literature: **Fortin et al., reported in *Lower
Extremity Review*'s "Over the Edge", measured a rigid boot taking 15° of plantarflexion and
10° of dorsiflexion off normal ankle motion.** A bare ankle gives about 45, so a boot
allows about **30** — three times the 10° in the model, which `model.md` was always honest
was a guess.

But raising the constant does not work, and Session 05 was right that it does not:

| point | waltz | spiral | extEdge | teapot | upright | sit | camel | over 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10° | 59 | 56 | 54 | 55 | 32 | 54 | **61!** | 1 |
| 25° | **73!** | 41 | **68!** | **69!** | 46 | 39 | 46 | 3 |

**The reason is not the number.** `bootDir` applied one constant to every free foot in
every frame, which makes it an identity — not *the boot allows no more than this* but
*every free foot is pointed exactly this hard, always*. Session 05 wrote that down and did
not act on it. Plantarflexion drives the blade wherever the shin already points, so one
number lifts the toe on a spiral and drives it at the ice on a landing. That is the whole
of the table.

**So it is two numbers and a field.** `ANKLE_MAX` 30° is what the boot allows;
`ANKLE_POINT` 10° is what an unauthored foot does, so **every pose written before today
draws exactly as it did** — verified, all eleven checkers green before the camel was
touched. A keyframe may now say `point`, in degrees, clamped to `ANKLE_MAX`; same shape as
`pitch` on a skating foot, and for the same reason.

`poseAt` interpolates it. Leaving it out of `lpP` would have made an authored point visible
in the keyframe and nowhere else, which is the failure that function's own comment warns
about, one field along.

`npm run ankle` prints both tables. A constant with a comment is a guess with a footnote;
the number and its measurement live together now. **Still not a coach's number** — a study
of injury is not a study of what a position looks like.

## The review — what else the ankle changed

Martyn's ask, after the change landed: go back over everything else and see what moves.

**The lunge, and the record was backwards.** `docs/model.md` had "there is no legal pose;
the boot only comes back inside 60° once the foot is lifted about 30 cm". Re-measured with
`point` free: at 10° there is still nothing, at any hip height or foot height — *stronger*
than the old claim, because with the trailing leg required to be extended rather than
tucked it never comes back at all. With the ankle free, legal poses appear **with the
trailing foot on the ice** and vanish as it lifts. Best per hip height, skating leg checked
too: hip 42 → 60°, 46 → 57°, 50 → 54°, 54 → 51°. **All 8,064 legal poses use a point of 8°
or less and half of them use zero** — the lunge was blocked by a constant that was too
*high*, and nothing in the file could ask for a flatter ankle than 10°. The pose is no
longer the blocker. **The rolled boot still is**, and it is now the only one.

**A limit on that measurement, worth knowing.** A free foot's shin-to-boot angle is `point`
by construction, so no checker can falsify it — `shin.mjs` iterates blades on the ice only,
and extending it to free feet would assert an identity. The trailing shin in those lunge
poses runs to ~39° against a cuff that allows 28, and nothing says so.

**Every existing pose has slack it is not using.** `npm run ankle`, and the split is the
whole argument for the field: a leg reaching backwards and **up** wants more point — spiral
56→36, sit spin 54→34, spiralCheck 56→36, all at 30° — and a leg reaching backwards and
**down** wants less — waltz 59→49 and extended edge 54→44, both at 0°. Four of the eight
moves sit within 6° of the limit with 10–20° of slack unused. **None of those numbers is a
judgement anybody made**: every pose written before today is at 10° because that was the
only number available. Its `point` is an unmade decision, not a choice. Not changed here —
it is a coach's question per pose.

**`freefoot.mjs` can finally fail for the reason its name gives.** Session 05's complaint
was that the boot-to-shin angle was fixed by construction, so "the boot holds the ankle
square" was unfalsifiable. It is authored now, so the file gained a second assertion: **no
authored `point` is past `ANKLE_MAX`**. `bootDir` clamps, and a clamp on its own is this
repository's oldest failure shape — a silent correction of somebody's number, the same as
the filter that named what it hid and the loop that discarded an authored hand. The clamp
stays; the assertion says nobody is relying on it. Broken on purpose: one key at 45° → 1
foot reported. Reads keyframes deliberately — an out-of-range number is an authoring
mistake and interpolating between two legal keys can never make one.

**The Ina Bauer and the spread eagle are unaffected.** Both feet are on the ice, so there is
no free boot and the ankle is irrelevant to them. They became *possible* on 30/08/2026 with
two blades and are still unbuilt for want of authoring, not for want of model.

## The BIS documents — a second ladder, and the two-generations trap again

Martyn downloaded everything on the open BIS site mid-session, twenty documents beyond the
Skills set. Read and triaged; `sources/bis/MANIFEST.md` now covers all forty.

**The National ladder is real, it is eight levels plus a Beginner, and it interlocks with
Skills.** Each level's entry requirement is the matching Skills level *plus* the National
level below — National 4 needs Skills 4 and National 3. `docs/gaps-competition.md` did not
know whether it was in scope; it plainly is, and the two ladders are not parallel.

**Two documents describe it three years apart and contradict each other on the elements.**
`Singles Generic Criteria 2026-2027` (09/06/2026) is current; `National Test Structure
3.7.23 V3` is superseded. Confirmed divergences at National 3 (2023 requires the spin with
no change of position to have a change of foot; 2026-27 requires **no** change of foot) and
National 4 (2023 requires the combination to show all three basic positions and the other
spin to be a Sit or Camel; neither survives). **Build against the 2026-27 document.** Same
trap the Skills generations already taught, and the same remedy — tell them apart by date.

**BIS define the three basic positions themselves, and on two of three they are more
specific than the ISU.** A sit requires **the free leg in front**; a camel requires **the
shoulders parallel to the ice**. The first is a clause `spin.mjs` could assert and does not
— the mirror of the camel's "backwards" clause it already has. The second cannot be
asserted: the shoulder line is built from `lateral(shYaw)` and is horizontal by
construction, so it is an identity, like a free foot's shin angle. They also restate the
ISU's counting rules independently — 3 revolutions for a spin, 2 for a position — which is
the best kind of confirmation.

**A discrepancy that must be settled before publishing either version.** `Singles Event
Structure 2526` disagrees with the championship table in `docs/gaps-competition.md` at three
rows of four: Basic Novice needs Skills 4 not 6, Advanced Novice 6 not 7, Junior 7 not 8.
The document's pattern is Skills N with National N. It looks like a transcription error in
that file, but the table was fetched as 2026/27 and the document is 2025-26, and **the
2026-27 event structure is not in `sources/`**. The table is flagged in place rather than
corrected.

**Twice while reading this I extracted the wrong level's requirements** — once from a
two-column layout and once from a page break that put a heading next to the previous level's
entry requirement. Both were caught by going back to a clean single-column block, and the
per-level summary was rebuilt mechanically rather than by eye. Anything read out of these
PDFs by hand should be assumed to be off by one until a block boundary confirms it.

## After the push — the spiral, the jumps, and the elements page

Three things Martyn asked for while looking at the live site.

### The spiral's free leg was bent, and now is not

*"The free leg is supposed to be extended not bent sharply at the knee."* He is right and it
had been that way since Session 01: **69% of reach, drawn knee 24 cm below the hip**, on a
position whose whole line is the extension. Now **93%, knee 5 cm below** — a soft knee rather
than a locked one, which is as straight as it gets without failing `reach.mjs`.

**It could not have been fixed before today.** Straightening the leg drives the free boot
down, and at the old fixed ankle of 10° the same straight leg reads **61°** against
`freefoot.mjs`'s limit of 60. The pose was bent because the alternative was illegal. At
`point: 25` it reads 50. That is what the `ANKLE_MAX` work bought, one page later.

**The free foot did not move up.** It sits where it always did, ~24 cm above the hip: the leg
was lengthened, not lifted.

**All three keys were solved together against every interpolated frame.** Two hand-written
attempts each produced a stretch over the limit — one at f=0.24, one at f=0.00 — which is
Session 12's waltz-jump fault exactly: a free leg whose extension changes between keys sweeps
its shin through horizontal, and a boot square to a horizontal shin points at the ice. Worst
frame now 50°. `spiralCheck` follows for free, since it clones the spiral's legs.

Authored near the boot's limit throughout, which is **a claim and not a measurement** — that a
skater in a spiral points the free foot as hard as the boot allows. First thing to put to a
coach.

### Six jump pages contained no picture at all

Not just no rig — **no tracing either**. Measured on the built HTML: of 251 element pages,
exactly seven carry neither an `<svg>` nor a body frame, and six are jumps. The seventh is
`other-names`, a listing. So every element page in the guide drew something except the jumps,
on a site whose own subtitle promises every jump drawn.

Jump pages now draw their **takeoff and landing** from the `jump:` block they already carry —
`EdgeThumb`, not `EdgeDiagram`, for the front page's reason: it draws in the frontmatter and
ships a path, where the full instrument costs a script and a rAF loop per copy.

**`shares` was a second copy of `confusableWith`.** The page imported the model's answer to
"which jumps share a takeoff edge" and then recomputed it by hand from its own frontmatter,
keyed on the jump's *name*. Same result today, drifting apart the first time either is
edited. Now it looks the key up and asks the model.

What separates a confusable pair is derived, not written: **either one uses the pick and the
other does not, or they differ in rotations.** There is no third answer, because the takeoff
edge is the only other thing a jump is made of. Waltz/Axel differ by rotations; Salchow/flip
and toe loop/loop by the pick; the Lutz is alone on LBO.

### The elements page has a contents strip

*"Very long with jumps at the bottom."* A derived strip of section links with counts, so the
bottom of the page is one tap rather than a scroll — and the jumps and positions lists now
carry a tracing each, which is most of why they read as an appendix.

**The strip is derived from the same counts the sections are.** A hand-written one would be a
second statement of what the page contains, and the last thing here that stated its own
contents by hand was the featured filter that silently absorbed twenty-four twizzles. The
compensating check asserts every element falls inside a section the strip names: 8 + 32 + 16 +
32 + 36 + 116 + 7 + 3 = **250**, all of them.

**The order is deliberately unchanged.** What should decide it is whether an element appears
in a test the guide holds — `docs/gaps-competition.md` sets that out and it is a bigger change
than a strip. Worth knowing before it is built: **Skills 1–8 contain no jumps and no spins**,
so that rule puts the jumps on the second tab, not the first.

### Compensating for two unbuildable .astro changes

No build in the bridge VM, so: frontmatter extracted and syntax-checked with `node --check`;

`_to_delete/verify-astro.mjs`, gitignored. That is a stand-in for a build and not a substitute

### The pick, specified and deliberately not built

Next after the jump tracings, and it turned out to be the size of the two-blades change rather
than the size of an afternoon: **four model and render files and five checkers**, enumerated in
`docs/model.md` under *The pick is a third kind of contact*. Starting it at the end of a session
this long is how a half-finished `onIce` gets left in the tree, so it is written up instead.

Two things found while scoping it. **`pick: true` already exists and is wrong** — a keyframe
flag that `blade.mjs` reads as exempting *every* on-ice blade in that frame, which is precisely

description waiting to be believed. And **`onIceOf` and `bladesDown` need no change at all**,
because the two-blades work already generalised them from "is this the skating foot" to "what
is this foot doing". The remaining cost is entirely in the places that still ask `=== 'blade'`
and mean *on the ice* — and those are found by grep, not by thought.
