# Nothing drawn may jump — and the free leg that was folding twice

**Session 11 · 30/08/2026** — Cowork. Follows Session 10. Two bug reports from Martyn on the
waltz jump, the six cluster pairings, and the checker that had to be invented because nothing
here could have found the first bug.

**254 elements, 316 pages. Thirteen checkers, ALL GREEN** — `check:freefoot` for the first
time since Session 05.

## The two reports were one fault, and it was the pose

Martyn: the top-down view sees the free boot rotate a couple of times towards the end of the
animation with no visible reason; the rear view shows it in side view pointing down, which the
leg position makes impossible.

The free leg's extension was authored inconsistently across the landing and run-out — **45%,
82%, 56%, 54%, 82%, 82%** of reach. Between keyframes the leg folded and unfolded twice, the
shin swept through **horizontal**, and `bootDir` builds a free boot square to the shin. A boot
square to a horizontal shin points straight at the ice. That is the identical failure that made
the lunge undrawable, written up in `docs/model.md` since Session 04, and `freefoot.mjs` had
been red on exactly those frames since Session 05.

### The near-miss, which is the part worth keeping

The top-down heading comes from the horizontal part of the boot direction, and that fell to
**0.071** of unit length. That looks exactly like the degenerate projection this repository has
now been bitten by three times, so the obvious fix was to derive the heading from the boot's
lateral axis instead — which is well defined by construction when the boot points down, and
which measured **rock steady at −9° through the entire stretch** while the direct heading swept
170°.

It would have been a lie. The boot genuinely pitches from toe-forward, through pointing at the
ice, to toe-backward; rotating about the lateral axis leaves that axis unchanged, which is
precisely why it looked stable. The renderer was drawing an impossible pose faithfully.

**A degenerate projection and a real motion look identical in one frame. Only the 3D vector
tells you which.** That is the lesson, and it nearly went the other way.

### The fix, and `freefoot.mjs` going green

Extension held near 90% of reach with the free foot's height following the hip, and the free leg
now passes **low** through the takeoff swing, close to the ice — which is both what fixes that
stretch and what a swing actually does. Nine numbers, in seven keyframes.

The solver was used to find the range and the numbers were then authored by hand. `model.md`'s
rule about optimisers editing poses has three failures on record and one of them moved a landing
foot half a metre to minimise this exact angle.

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| frames over the 60° limit | 85, in four stretches | **0** |
| worst free-boot elevation | 85.9° | 59.1° |
| min horizontal part of the boot direction | 0.071 | 0.571 |

`freefoot.mjs` has been deliberately red since Session 05, sitting last in the chain so one
honest red would not stop the others reporting. Its header said the fix was the pose or the
limit and that neither was a developer's to choose. **It was the pose, in all four stretches**,
and `ANKLE_FREE` never needed touching. It stays last in the chain — a pose check is where poses
fail, and the argument holds again if it goes red.

## `tools/continuity.mjs`, the thirteenth checker

Martyn asked the right question: can this be checked before he eyeballs it?

**Nothing in the repository could have found the spin.** Every checker here asserts a property
of one frame — is the blade on the ice, is the shin inside the boot, does the lobe curve the
right way — and a glyph that is correct in every frame and wrong *between* them passes all
twelve. A whole missing class.

The rig interpolates smoothly, so everything drawn is a continuous function of the clock. A jump
between adjacent frames is one of three things and the file says which: a **degenerate
projection**, a **branch change**, or a **pose nothing could skate**.

**11,514 adjacent-frame comparisons**, six moves, three views, read off the markup the renderer
actually produced rather than a re-derivation. That needed `data-boot`, `data-foot`,
`data-heading` and `data-horiz` adding to the top-down glyph — nothing could read it, so nothing
could check it, which is the same argument that put `data-limb` and `data-roll` on the profile
views.

**Broken on purpose with the real bug.** Restoring the old free-leg keyframes gives 5
discontinuities, the first being *"waltz top L: the glyph turns 155° between frames 136 and
137"*. Injecting a random 180° flip into the heading gives 144.

### What it reports rather than failing

Both are design questions, so it prints numbers and leaves them:

- **The rear view spends 110 to 132 frames of about 192 on a near-tie** between the side-on and
  end-on boot glyph, on four of the six moves. **More than half of every held position is drawn
  in the ambiguous branch.** That is the second half of Martyn's report and it is now a
  measurement rather than an impression. It is the same open item as *a boot seen from above its
  own opening*, which still has no glyph — and this is the first time anyone has known how many
  frames need one.
- The glyph switches branch ten times in the waltz's rear view.

## The pairings — so the animation shows the join

Martyn's call, and his reason: a skater should see one turn run into the next, which a cluster
page gives them and two separate pages do not.

Six runs the syllabus writes as one thing, each previously listed as its parts with a
`notCovered` line: `double-three-mohawk`, `rocker-mohawk`, `bracket-crossroll`,
`counter-two-and-a-half-twizzle`, `three-coe-double-twizzle`, `rocker-counter-double-twizzle`.
**48 pages, six gap lines closed.**

**A cluster may now hold a change of edge, a cross roll and a twizzle**, not only turns and
steps. `buildTrace` needed **no changes at all** — the chain loop already joined all five kinds,
because `join` was stored on the element rather than inferred from whether it was a turn. That
decision was made for the transitions in an earlier session and it paid here in full.

Every one of the six lands on the edge BIS's own next numbered step names, with nothing told:
LBI bracket-cross roll → RFO, and their sequence reads `(10)LBI bracket-cross roll (11)RFO`.

`tracing.mjs`'s `CHAINS` was a hand-kept copy of `CLUSTERS` and is now derived from it. 208
combinations rather than 184 — and a hand-kept list would have checked none of the six.

## Two holes closed on the way past

**A bare `notCovered:` is null, not an empty list.** Removing the last gap line from four
exercises left the key with nothing under it. Astro rejects the file; every tool here reads it as
empty, because they parse frontmatter with regexes. `syllabus.mjs` passed and the build failed on
the Mac. The build is the only real YAML validator in the chain and it is the one step that
cannot run in the bridge VM, so `syllabus.mjs` now asserts it directly.

**Assertion 5 had its own blind spot.** Its predicates checked `e.turn` alone for everything but
the three turns, so the six new clusters — which carry their twizzle or cross roll in `turns` —
made it report exercises that had just been made *more* complete. One helper, `has(e, k)`,
checking both.

## Two things the dev server found that nothing else did

Martyn could not load the site for testing. Two separate causes, and the second is the more
important.

**The dev server was wedged.** Up for two hours twenty-three minutes, through two
`content.config.ts` schema changes and seventy-two new content files, and it had stopped
answering on both loopback addresses. `npx astro dev stop`, `rm -rf .astro`, restart. The URL
also has a base path — `http://localhost:4321/figureskating/`, not `/` — which is now recorded
in state-of-play where someone will look for it.

**Restarting it surfaced four dangling content references, and `npm run check` was green.**
`astro build` logs an invalid content reference as an error and **exits zero**; `links.mjs` had
nothing to find, because the broken reference never reached the HTML.

The bug was mine, from the pairings an hour earlier. **A cluster is only real if every element
in it is real from the state it starts at.** A bracket changes edge, so from an outside entry it
exits on an inside one — and there is no such thing as a cross roll from an inside edge. Four of
the eight `bracket-crossroll` entries named a prerequisite with no page. BIS write it once, as
*LBI bracket-cross roll*: an inside entry, which is the half that is legal.

Fixed as a rule rather than four files: `gen-derived.mjs` refuses to write a cluster whose chain
reaches a state where its next element does not exist, and `syllabus.mjs` gained assertion 1b —
every prerequisite resolves — because the build will not do it. Broken on purpose; it reports.

**The lesson to keep: a green check chain is not a green build, and a green build is not a
loading site.** Three different things, and only the last one is what a reader gets.

## Decisions locked

- **A degenerate projection and a real motion look identical in one frame.** Check the 3D vector
  before "fixing" a projection, or you will conceal the fault you were sent to find.
- **Nothing drawn may jump.** Per-frame correctness is not enough; the class between frames is
  real and now has a checker.
- **A fact the renderer knows belongs in the DOM.** The top-down glyph was unreadable and
  therefore uncheckable, and that is where the bug lived.
- **`join` on the element, not inferred.** Six clusters containing three new kinds of join
  needed no renderer change at all.
- **Report, do not fail, where judgement is required.** Branch flips and degenerate-zone
  occupancy are printed, not enforced.
