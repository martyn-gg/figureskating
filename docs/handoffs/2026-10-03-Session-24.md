## The Salchow

Martyn is working towards it. The rig starts checked on LBI, coming out of the three turn:

- The free leg is held back, swung wide and through.
- It takes off with no pick and does one rotation in the air.
- It lands with the waltz jump's RBO landing, 360° further round.

`hipYaw` runs 202 to 540 and is never wrapped, because `poseAt` interpolates the raw number.
Every checker passes.

**The three turn was tried first and failed four checkers for one reason:** a one-foot turn
pivots a gripping blade at the cusp.

- `dir` is a carried state. Flipping it swings the boot 179° between two frames, which fails
  `continuity`.
- Turning the hip ahead of a gripping blade asks for more toe-in than the leg allows, which fails
  `turnout`.
- `shin` and `lean` failed for the same reason.

That is step 2 of the plan.

**For a coach**, the two things to put to them:

- The hips turn only about 25° on the ice before the blade leaves, as far as a gripping blade
  allows; the shoulders lead by more. Does that match how the takeoff should look?
- The legs are tucked at the peak, as on the waltz jump, where a real Salchow is closer and
  straighter.

## Step 1: the content pass, brief for the next session

**The standard is `forward-stroking`:**

- what the skater does;
- what it feels like;
- the usual fault, named plainly ("never backwards off the toe").

Use what a governing body's public text supports. For the basics that is Learn to Skate USA; for
anything in Skills it is BIS. Do not invent mechanics. Where a claim needs a coach, leave it out or
say so.

**Order:**

1. The 22 basics and the slip step: the beginners' pages, where the review found the prose most
   generic.
2. Then the one-foot turns.
3. Then the two-foot turns and the transitions.

The generated families (turns, transitions, twizzles, clusters) share passages across eight
entries each. Rewrite the passage once and apply it to every file that carries it, as
`_to_delete/apply_audit.py` did for the copy pass.

**Cluster titles:** give them human titles, for example "LFO rocker, counter, double twizzle".
Keep the full generated name in `aliases` so search still finds it.

**Rules that hold:**

- `docs/style.md`: second person on element pages, 90 to 160 words (60 to 160 derived).
- Foot-neutral.
- The new writing rules, which `house.mjs` now enforces.

Run `house`, `prose` and `drift` after each batch, and the full chain before committing.

**`tools/gen-derived.mjs` still carries the pre-audit wording.** A `--force` regeneration would
fail `house.mjs`, so it is safe, but it is stale. Bring its templates in line when the generated
prose is rewritten.

## Step 2: the three turn, notes for whoever builds it

- `twoFootTurn` already does the related thing for a skid. It keeps `dir` constant and puts the
  whole 180° in the per-foot `yaw`, so the blade heading is travel plus yaw and nothing jumps.
- A one-foot three turn needs that on a **gripping** blade over the few frames around the cusp.
  `turnout.mjs` currently refuses a yawed blade claiming to grip, so it needs a declared
  exception that holds the cusp to account from both sides (see the `new-checker` skill: "an
  exemption can only excuse a pose").
- Then the tracing must show the cusp, and the edge letter changes O to I at it.
- When it works, prepend LFO plus the three turn to `MOVES.salchow`. It was written that way
  first, so it is a path segment and three keys.
