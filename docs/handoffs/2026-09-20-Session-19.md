## The two basics were one true claim and one false one

Session 18 left the drag and backward stroking as "the only two basics waiting on nothing but
authoring", with a warning to measure the drag's trailing foot first. The warning was right
to exist and wrong about which wall.

**Backward stroking was waiting on nothing but authoring.** `backStroke` is `pushOff` with the
pelvis facing back down the ice. The yaw of the pushing foot is **the same number in both**,
which looks like it should not be: `bootDir` takes a planted blade's heading from the
direction of travel, 0 forwards and 180 backwards, plus the authored yaw — so going backwards
flips the base, and facing backwards flips which side of the track is the skater's right
because `lateral(180)` is `-n`. The two flips cancel. `turnout.mjs` reads both feet at exactly
35° out. Same property `twoFootTurnBack` found in the yaws of a turn, arriving by the same
route.

What is not mirrored is the body, and that is the half the page is about. Every syllabus
teaches this long after the forward version and not because of the feet, so the shoulder lead
and the forward weight are authored rather than left to the mirror: `sh.t` negative, over the
toes and not the heels. A skater sitting back on a backward push has the blade run away in
front of them, and that is a shape this rig can draw.

**The drag was not**, and the reason took three corrections to reach.

## Three things had to be settled before a number meant anything

**Which way is behind.** `t` is the direction of TRAVEL, so behind the skater is `-t` at
hipYaw 0 and `+t` at hipYaw 180. The first sweep used `+t` at hipYaw 0, which is in FRONT of
the skater. It reported the region as benign — because it is — and would have had me author
keyframes into a wall. `moves.js` opens by warning about exactly this.

**Which sign is up, and this one was a real fault in the repository.** `freefoot.mjs`, which
owns the 60° limit, reads elevation as `asin(bd[2])`, positive toe up. `ankle.mjs` reads
`asin(-bd[2])` and takes the absolute value of it everywhere. **Two expressions of one
quantity with opposite signs, hidden by an `abs()`, since `ankle.mjs` was written.** Fixed,
with the output diffed before and after to prove every printed number unchanged — which is
the point: it was latent, and the next reader who wanted the sign rather than the magnitude
would have had it backwards. It surfaced only because `model.md`'s camel table was read back
against a fresh sweep and came out mirrored.

**What `point` the camel surface was swept at.** It reproduces at **point 25, n −8, hip 96**,
four of five landmarks within a degree and the fifth inside the range it was quoted as. That
is a pointed foot, not the default 10. The elevation surface is a function of the ankle as
well as the leg, so a table read at a different point is a different table. Worth writing on
the table itself, and now is.

## What blocks the drag, corrected mid-session by Martyn

The first version of this section measured a trailing **blade, flat on the ice, turned out** —
which is what the element page said — and concluded `shin.mjs` was the wall. Martyn: **a
drag's free boot is turned IN, the skating knee sinks until the free blade lifts clear, and
what trails is the INSIDE OF THE BOOT.**

So the contact is not a blade, and putting the boot's side down means **rolling** it. That is
the axis `docs/model.md` has said the rig has not got since Session 14 and the one that has
blocked the lunge ever since. **The drag is not a new blocker. It is the lunge's.**

**The model was already saying the blade comes up**, and this was measured before the
correction arrived, so it reads as corroboration rather than a patch:

| hip | furthest back with the marker on the ice | the blade's low end | lift needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 96 | 32 cm | −8.9 cm | 9.5 cm |
| 92 | 42 cm | −9.9 cm | 10.5 cm |
| 88 | 50 cm | −10.6 cm | 11.0 cm |
| 84 | 56 cm | −11.1 cm | 12.0 cm |

And lifting buys reach: 50 cm back at a hip of 88 with the marker on the surface, 64 at ten
centimetres up, 75 at twenty. Sink, extend, and the blade has to leave the ice. The first pass
read those negative numbers as a pose being blocked; they are the pose saying where the foot
goes. **Read a negative number twice before calling it a wall.**

### The footnote that survives

The pose as the page described it is not drawable either, and it is worth keeping because it
is what the guide would have shipped. A boot allows 28° of shin lean:

| hip | skating blade | trailing, 20 cm | 30 cm | 40 cm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 96 | 23° | 26° | 25° | out of reach |
| 94 | 26° | **31°** | **32°** | out of reach |
| 92 | **30°** | **35°** | **37°** | **33°** |
| 88 | **36°** | **41°** | **44°** | **44°** |

Pitching the boot makes it worse monotonically — 44° at zero, 52° at 8, 64° at 20, 80° at 40 —
because tilting the boot tilts its up-axis away from the shin. And `turnout.mjs` reads a
trailing toe as 150° of hip rotation, the case its own header predicted and excluded picks for.

## The checker, and what it found

`tools/underice.mjs` — **a boot that is not on the ice is not drawn below it.** The class of
fault nothing could see, and the deciding one twice now.

It is not covered by `freefoot.mjs`, which limits the boot's ANGLE: the angle is half of where
a boot ends up and the height is the other half, and a boot 30 cm long pointed at the 60°
limit reaches 14 cm below its marker. It measures off the **real render tree** rather than off
the pose, because Session 15's version of this fault was a plan glyph that failed to pivot
about its contact and no correct pose data would have saved it. Where the ice is comes out of
the markup — `data-ice`, added for it.

| mutation | runs |
|---|---|
| clean | 9 over 6,490 glyphs |
| `--break=sink` every free foot 20 cm lower | 20, deepest 14.4 cm |
| `--break=flat` every free foot at z 0 | 30, deepest 17.0 cm |
| `--break=blind` the on-ice exemption removed | 66 over 24,384 glyphs |

A fourth was tried and thrown away, recorded in the header because it is worth knowing:
pointing every free foot to `ANKLE_MAX` took the count **down**, 9 to 6, since plantarflexion
drives the blade wherever the shin already points. A mutation that reduces the count has not
broken anything.

**It dictated a fix rather than confirming one.** Three of the nine are one fault: a boot
built square to the shin gives a free leg sloping down and forward a foot at a right angle to
it — toe up, **heel down** — and it is the heel going through the ice, 5.4 cm of it for
seventy-five consecutive frames on the sit spin, at a boot angle of 58.6° comfortably inside
`freefoot.mjs`'s 60. **An extended free leg is pointed.** `POINTED` at 25, inside `ANKLE_MAX`,
on the keys where the leg is extended and not on the entrance, wind-up and exit. 42 of 399
hashed frames moved, all in the three moves touched.

**It is out of the `check` chain, beside `drift`**, and that is a debt rather than a design.
Two runs survive: the waltz jump's landing, which pointing makes worse, and the change of
foot, whose free foot is at z 0 at the instant it becomes the skating foot — the step-over
this rig does not draw, after `twoFoot` and `toePick`.

## Decisions locked

- **A drag's contact is the side of the boot, not a blade.** Martyn. It needs a boot that can
  roll, which is the lunge's missing axis and a different one from what the camel change
  wants. The SKID-not-PUSH question this session settled first is moot: there is no blade on
  the ice to declare.
- **An extended free leg is pointed** — `POINTED` 25 in `moves.js`, on the keys where the leg
  is extended.
- **`underice.mjs` stays out of the chain until the waltz landing and the change of foot are
  settled**, then goes in with a declared exemption list like `drawn.mjs`'s.
- **`drawn.mjs` no longer states how many pages remain undrawable.** It said ten while the
  list held nine. Fifth time a hand-stated count in this repository has drifted from the thing
  it counts; the run prints it from the list.
- **`ankle.mjs` uses `freefoot.mjs`'s sign for elevation.**
