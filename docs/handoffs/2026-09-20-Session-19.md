## The two basics were one true claim and one false one

Session 18 left the drag and backward stroking as "the only two basics waiting on nothing but
authoring", with a warning to measure the drag's trailing foot first. The warning was right
to exist and wrong about which wall.

**Backward stroking was waiting on nothing but authoring.** `backStroke` is `pushOff` with the
pelvis facing back down the ice. The yaw of the pushing foot is **the same number in both**,
which looks like it should not be: `bootDir` takes a planted blade's heading from the
direction of travel, 0 forwards and 180 backwards, plus the authored yaw — so going backwards
flips the base, and facing backwards flips which side of the track is the skater's right
because `lateral(180)` is `-n`. The two flips cancel. `turnout.mjs` reads both feet at exactly
35° out. Same property `twoFootTurnBack` found in the yaws of a turn, arriving by the same
route.

What is not mirrored is the body, and that is the half the page is about. Every syllabus
teaches this long after the forward version and not because of the feet, so the shoulder lead
and the forward weight are authored rather than left to the mirror: `sh.t` negative, over the
toes and not the heels. A skater sitting back on a backward push has the blade run away in
front of them, and that is a shape this rig can draw.

**The drag was not**, and the reason took three corrections to reach.

## Three things had to be settled before a number meant anything

**Which way is behind.** `t` is the direction of TRAVEL, so behind the skater is `-t` at
hipYaw 0 and `+t` at hipYaw 180. The first sweep used `+t` at hipYaw 0, which is in FRONT of
the skater. It reported the region as benign — because it is — and would have had me author
keyframes into a wall. `moves.js` opens by warning about exactly this.

**Which sign is up, and this one was a real fault in the repository.** `freefoot.mjs`, which
owns the 60° limit, reads elevation as `asin(bd[2])`, positive toe up. `ankle.mjs` reads
`asin(-bd[2])` and takes the absolute value of it everywhere. **Two expressions of one
quantity with opposite signs, hidden by an `abs()`, since `ankle.mjs` was written.** Fixed,
with the output diffed before and after to prove every printed number unchanged — which is
the point: it was latent, and the next reader who wanted the sign rather than the magnitude
would have had it backwards. It surfaced only because `model.md`'s camel table was read back
against a fresh sweep and came out mirrored.

**What `point` the camel surface was swept at.** It reproduces at **point 25, n −8, hip 96**,
four of five landmarks within a degree and the fifth inside the range it was quoted as. That
is a pointed foot, not the default 10. The elevation surface is a function of the ankle as
well as the leg, so a table read at a different point is a different table. Worth writing on
the table itself, and now is.

## What blocks the drag

**Not the elevation surface.** The camel's bad region is mid-height behind, `z` 30 to 70. A
drag is on the ice, where a pointed free foot reads −31° ten centimetres behind and −54° at
fifty — inside 60 at every hip height down to 88.

**A boot under the ice, which nothing checks.** As a free foot the boot's low end sits 7 to
11 cm BELOW `z` 0, because a boot square to a shin that steep points into the ice and the
glyph is 26 cm long. Session 15's fault exactly. `freefoot.mjs` judges the boot's angle and
**nothing judges where its ends finish up.** A checker is owed; it is on the list below.

**`shin.mjs`, and the element's own content walks into it.** A boot allows 28° of lean:

| hip | skating blade | trailing, 20 cm | 30 cm | 40 cm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 96 | 23° | 26° | 25° | out of reach |
| 94 | 26° | **31°** | **32°** | out of reach |
| 92 | **30°** | **35°** | **37°** | **33°** |
| 88 | **36°** | **41°** | **44°** | **44°** |

Only a locked skating leg keeps both shins legal, and then the trail caps at about 30 cm.
Every hip from 94 down puts the **skating** blade alone over before the trailing foot is
considered. The page's own sentence — the skating knee stays bent — is the thing that cannot
be drawn.

**And pitching the boot makes it worse, monotonically.** The obvious move is to point the
foot, since a real drag's ankle is plantarflexed. 44° at zero pitch becomes 52° at 8, 64° at
20, 80° at 40. Tilting the boot tilts its up-axis away from the shin, so the quantity being
measured grows with the thing meant to relieve it.

**`turnout.mjs` blocks it a second time and predicted itself.** A trailing toe points back
down the ice, and the checker reads the raw plan angle as 150° of hip rotation. Its header
already says why it cannot tell the difference: *a toe pointing away from the body is hip
EXTENSION with a pointed ankle*. Picks are excluded for that reason; a dragged blade does the
same thing and is not.

## Decisions locked

- **A drag's trailing foot is a SKID and not a PUSH**, settled in advance of it ever being
  drawn. A push drives sideways and grips; a blade dragged along the line of travel while
  pointing off it is sliding across itself. The one word that will not fit is `SKID()`'s own
  "weight on it" — a drag is the first skid that is not a stop, and the comment is what should
  change rather than the kind.
- **`drawn.mjs` no longer states how many pages remain undrawable.** It said ten while the
  list held nine. Fifth time a hand-stated count in this repository has drifted from the thing
  it counts; the run prints it from the list.
- **`ankle.mjs` uses `freefoot.mjs`'s sign for elevation.**
