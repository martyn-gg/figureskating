## The ankle sat behind the blade centre and was applied to the contact

The first fix, and the widest. `ankleOf` set the ankle back from the foot's origin by
`ANKLE_BACK`, and the origin moved to whatever was touching the ice the moment the pick
arrived. On a blade the two coincide; on a pick they are 17.8 cm apart, and the side view
drew the shin entering the boot 17.8 cm behind the laces.

`contactAlongOf(pose, which, bd)` now says how far along the runner the contact is, and
`ankleOf` subtracts it as well as `ANKLE_BACK`. **Ankle-to-cuff went from 5.1–17.5 cm to
5.1 cm on every foot in the rig** — it was never a pick-only fault; the picked pose was
simply the first one big enough to see.

Three spin poses failed the moment the ankle moved, which is the fix working. Upright and
camel were corrected by **raising the hip two centimetres** — the hip stays on the spin axis
and the sweep stays at 0 cm. The sit spin would not take that: at a hip of 54 the height
moves lean by 0.6° over four centimetres, and at the last key it moved it the wrong way, so
**the foot moved instead**. Worth remembering which lever a spin takes; it is not the same
one twice, and `docs/model.md` now says so.

## Twenty-two basics, and what they cost the model

Martyn: *the guide lacks basic techniques like stroking.* He was right, and the gap was
structural rather than an oversight — the guide started at the edges because the rig started
at the edges. Learn to Skate USA Basic 1–6 is the anchor (two independent club copies, since
learntoskateusa.com would not serve the syllabus), and the whole account is in
`docs/gaps-basics.md`.

**Fourteen of the twenty-two could not be drawn at all**, and all fourteen for one reason:
the rig took a blade's heading from the tracing, because a blade on the ice can only lie
along its own line. That is true of a glide and false of everything a beginner is actually
taught — a push, a stop, a two-foot turn. So the model gained two things:

**Per-foot `yaw`.** A blade on the ice may point somewhere other than where it is going, and
what bounds it is anatomy, not the ice: `HIP_OUT` 40°, `HIP_IN` 20°, plus knee twist that
grows with flexion (`KNEE_TWIST_OUT` 18°, `KNEE_TWIST_IN` 25°, `KNEE_TWIST_FULL` 35°).
`tools/turnout.mjs` is the twentieth checker and measures blade heading against the pelvis,
with the allowance computed per pose from the knee.

**`onIce: 'skid'`**, the fourth contact. A blade sliding across itself rather than along
itself: it draws a contact bar rather than a ring, and the tracing gains a third state — a
scrape band whose width is `|sin(yaw)| × (BLADE_FRONT − BLADE_BACK)`, so it is widest at 90°
and vanishes at nought.

**I asserted a skid must be flat, and that was wrong.** `SKID_FLAT` lasted about twenty
minutes. The web is unanimous that a T-stop rides the trailing blade's **outside** edge —
riding the inside is the classic fault coaches correct — and a hockey stop has both blades
tilted. Replaced with `SKID_MIN_YAW` (15°) and an authored edge that the checker insists a
skid names.

Eight basics now carry a tracing: `trace` on the element, `PathThumb.astro` to draw it.
`tools/drawn.mjs` is the nineteenth checker — every element page draws something, or is named
in `cannotDraw` with a reason, and it fails **both ways**, so a page that starts drawing while
still declared undrawable is a failure too.

## `lpP` dropped `yaw` and `edge`, and I reported the push working before measuring it

The worst half-hour of the session and the one worth keeping. Per-foot yaw went in, the push
was authored, I rendered it, looked at the picture, and said it worked. It did not: `lpP`
interpolates a foot between keyframes field by field and it had never been taught about `yaw`
or `edge`, so **every frame between keys drew the blade running true**. The one frame I looked
at happened to be a key.

`lpP`'s own comment had predicted exactly this — a field-by-field interpolator silently drops
anything added later. `tools/twofoot.mjs` now round-trips `FOOT_FIELDS = ['onIce','dir','edge',
'yaw','pitch','point']` through `poseAt` **by name**, so the next field to arrive is caught by
the file that already exists.

**Render, don't reason — and then measure, don't eye.** The repository's rule said the first
half. The second half is new and it is in `docs/model.md`.

## The house style, asserted

`tools/house.mjs` is the twenty-first checker: banned words, banned openers, US spellings, an
em-dash budget, foot neutrality — the guide never assumes which foot a reader pushes off — and
a word-count floor. **Its first run reported 167 violations**, 123 of them word count.

Most of that was the checker being blunt rather than the prose being bad, and narrowing it is
the work: `just` is exempt when temporal or a quantity, `easy` when comparative, the word floor
splits 60 for derived pages and 90 for hand-written, and a plain-edge summary is exempt from
foot neutrality because naming the foot *is* the element. **The fixes went into
`gen-derived.mjs`, not into the 244 files it writes** — `easy` and foot-naming came out of the
templates, and loop, Axel, toe loop and Salchow gained third paragraphs. Regenerate with
`node tools/gen-derived.mjs --force`.

## Martyn was right about the hockey stop

I had written that there is no legal pose for a hockey stop and left it reading as a claim
about skaters. He asked whether the boot restriction was a figure-skate-versus-hockey-skate
artefact. It is not — Learn to Skate USA teaches the hockey stop in Basic 5, in figure skates,
and a hockey boot buys five to nine degrees of ankle, not the missing thirty — but the claim
was still overreaching, because **the failure is a rig limit, not a boot limit**. A hockey stop
is made of counter-rotation and a free upper body, and the model has one hip yaw, a knee facing
wherever that yaw points, and no spine. Corrected in `tools/drawn.mjs` and `docs/model.md`.

The general form, and it belongs with Session 15's: when a sweep finds no legal pose, say which
of the model's absences produced that answer. "Cannot" without a named absence reads as a claim
about the sport.
