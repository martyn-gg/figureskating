A long back outside edge, the right toe pick placed behind, and the vault over the
pick sends you into a rotation that runs *against* the curve you have been tracing.
That counter-rotation is what makes it feel different from every other jump.

It is also why it goes wrong. Every other jump shares its takeoff edge with another
jump; the Lutz has that edge to itself. Let the shoulders come round early and the
edge rolls from outside to inside — at which point you have jumped a flip and called
it a Lutz. The fault is visible long before the takeoff, in the shoulder line.
