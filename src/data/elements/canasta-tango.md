Both partners skate the same fourteen steps in reversed Kilian hold, all of them forwards. It is
the dance that introduces the tango rhythm, and US Figure Skating singles out three steps: the
chassé at step 4 and the slide chassés at steps 7 and 10, where knee action is what carries the
tango feel. Step 14 may start with a cross roll. The push for it comes from a stroke of the
other foot, and a push off the toe is what the rulebook asks skaters to avoid.

British Ice Skating sets it at National 2 for pattern dance with the Dutch Waltz. New Zealand's
KiwiSkate assesses it on the second dance badge, with forward progressives in its timing. US
Figure Skating calls it the Tango Canasta.
