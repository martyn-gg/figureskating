Both feet are on the ice, one travelling forwards and the other backwards, on two tracings
that run side by side. That second tracing is the difference from the spread eagle in US
Figure Skating's definitions, where both feet share one. Its ice dance glossary adds where the
weight goes: on the front foot, with the back foot extended behind the front foot's tracing.

Ice Skating Australia asks for about two metres, on a curve or a straight line, at Free Skate 4:
the front leg bent, the back leg extended on an inside edge, the back kept upright, and the
movement done on each side. New Zealand's KiwiSkate assesses the inside Ina Bauer at Free
skating 2, with a spread eagle accepted in its place. Like the spread eagle, it is one of the
movements the national tests in Britain and the United States name for a choreographic
sequence.
