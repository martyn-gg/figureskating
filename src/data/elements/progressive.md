The free foot passes the skating foot before it is placed on the ice, which leaves the foot you
were skating on trailing behind the new one. In the dances it is usually outside then inside on
the same lobe, and the ISU asks for the feet to be lifted a small distance from the ice. In the
Dutch Waltz it takes two beats, one, then three.

The Fourteenstep's key points rule out skating it as a chassé: a chassé puts the new foot down
beside the skating foot, a progressive puts it ahead. The ISU and U.S. Figure Skating also call
it a run or a crossover. This guide's crossover is the stroking step, where the free foot
crosses the skating foot completely, which is how U.S. Figure Skating's own solo dance list
defines a crossover.

KiwiSkate asks for forward progressives at Dance 1 and backward ones at Dance 5.
