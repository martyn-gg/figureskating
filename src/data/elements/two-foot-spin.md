Both blades stay on the ice and turn together on the spot, the feet close, while the arms and
upper body drive the rotation. Ice Skating Australia asks for the skates to stay together: one
revolution at first, then two, then three from an entry of two or three backward crossovers,
finishing on a back outside edge with the free leg extended. Learn to Skate USA has a beginning
two-foot spin of up to four revolutions at its fourth level and an advanced one of at least four
at its fifth. KiwiSkate teaches it from a spiralling edge.

Australia's measure of a good one is that it stays put: no more than three blade lengths from
where the first revolution started. U.S. Figure Skating treats it as a different spin from any
one-foot spin, and lets it count as one of the two spins on its Pre-Preliminary test.
