Gliding backwards on both feet, the body rotates through a half turn and the skater finishes
gliding forwards. Usually first learned on a circle, where the curve helps carry the turn
round.

It is the harder direction, and the reason is what you can see rather than what the feet are
doing: the rotation ends with the skater facing a stretch of ice they have not been looking
at. The turn tends to be rushed for that reason, and a rushed turn is one where the feet
snap round ahead of the body and the blades scrape. Turning the head first and letting the
shoulders follow keeps it in order.

Together with the forward version this is how a skater changes direction at will, which is
the point at which the ice starts to feel navigable rather than one-way.
