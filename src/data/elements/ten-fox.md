The Ten-Fox borrows from the Foxtrot and from the Tenstep, the social dance that grew into the 14 Step, and it changes hold several times: waltz hold to start, foxtrot hold for Steps 5 to 7, waltz hold again, then an offset closed hold across the ends with shoulders flat to the tracings.

The rulebook singles out Step 4, where the lead partner's two-beat change of edge has to coincide with the follow partner stepping forward, knees bending together. At Step 8 the follow partner rises and bends in time with the lead partner's three-turn, and after the open choctaw the lead partner checks rotation. US Figure Skating wants the glide to look effortless.

The Ten-Fox is not on the British Ice Skating national pattern dance list. It is on the KiwiSkate Dance 6 badge in New Zealand, and US Figure Skating tests it at Bronze.
