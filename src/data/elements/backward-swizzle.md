Both blades stay on the ice and you travel backwards: toes together, press the heels apart,
then draw them back together, the forward swizzle's lemon shape drawn the other way along.
Learn to Skate USA asks for six to eight in a row at its second level, after backward wiggles
at its first, and Ice Skating Australia asks for the same count. New Zealand calls it backward
two-foot sculling.

The fault the Australian manual names is the skater slowing down and pressing the toe picks
into the ice. Its answer is knee bend and posture: bend the knees and stay upright over the
skates, so that the push comes from the blades and not the picks. Wiggles come first in all
three programmes, a zigzag backwards on both feet with the upper body twisting while the head
and arms stay still.
