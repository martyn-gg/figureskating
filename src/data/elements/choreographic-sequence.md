A choreographic sequence is the part of a programme built for the music: at least two
different skating movements, such as spirals, arabesques, spread eagles, Ina Bauers or
hydroblading, joined by steps and turns. British Ice Skating and U.S. Figure Skating define it
the same way. Above the lowest levels it may also hold spins and jumps of up to two
revolutions, but none of them is called as an element of its own. The pattern is free as long
as it can be seen.

It has a fixed base value and the judges mark only its quality (GOE). British Ice Skating
relaxes it at Beginner, National 1 and National 2: one movement is enough, jumps and spins are
not allowed in it, and it has to cover at least two thirds of the ice. At those levels it
scores half the full value.
