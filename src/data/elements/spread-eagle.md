Both feet stay on the ice with the heels towards each other and the toes pointing opposite
ways, so one blade travels forwards and the other backwards, and both follow the same curve.
US Figure Skating defines it in exactly those terms: a forward edge and the matching backward
edge, on one tracing. Its rulebook allows the pair to be outside edges or inside edges, as long
as the two match.

Ice Skating Australia asks for the inside edges at Free Skate 2: at least a metre on a curve,
both knees straight, in both directions. New Zealand's KiwiSkate teaches the inside spread
eagle at Novice 2 and accepts it at Free skating 2 in place of the Ina Bauer. British and US tests
name it for choreographic sequences.

In the Ina Bauer the two feet travel on parallel tracings, with the back leg extended behind.
