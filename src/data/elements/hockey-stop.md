Travelling forwards, both feet rotate together to a right angle across the direction of
travel and the blades skid sideways, throwing ice, until the skater stops. It is the
shortest stop there is.

The rotation is not the difficulty. The difficulty is that both blades have to be flat
enough to skid rather than bite, and that requires letting the ankles roll while the body
stays upright and stacked over the skates — which is precisely the instinct a skater has
spent every previous month learning to suppress. Leaning back to protect against the
deceleration is what makes the edges catch, and catching an edge at that angle is how people
get hurt.

Worth saying plainly: this one is genuinely hard, it takes most people a long time, and
being able to do everything either side of it is normal.
