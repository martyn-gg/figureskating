A loop that lands on the other foot. It leaves the back outside edge the way a loop does,
turns once, and comes down on the back inside edge of what was the free foot.

That landing is the whole point of it. The back inside edge is where the Salchow and the
flip take off, so an Euler in the middle of a combination lets a skater follow almost any
jump with one of those two. In competition it counts only there, between two jumps, and
only as a single.

It used to be called the half loop, and some coaches still call it that. Land it with the
free leg held behind, the way any landing is held, and the next jump is already set up.
