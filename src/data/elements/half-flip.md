You take off from a back inside edge with the other foot's toe pick, turn half way in the air
away from the picking foot, and land on the toe of the other foot before the picking foot
comes down. Ice Skating Australia then asks for a forward inside three turn and the landing
position held on a back outside edge. Its manual calls it a split jump without the split.

The landing is what to check: it comes down on the toe of the foot that did not pick, never the
same one. U.S. Figure Skating's list of jumps calls it the half toe Salchow, a half-revolution
jump, and counts it as a jump on the Pre-Preliminary test but not in Preliminary competition.

New Zealand's KiwiSkate assesses it at Free skating 2.
