A plain edge, held. Time is what turns it from the gap between two elements into an element:
British Ice Skating asks for a third of a circle, or three seconds, before anything else is
allowed to happen. Three seconds is a long while to do nothing on one foot.

There is nothing to hide behind. On a turn a mistake arrives and leaves again; here it
accumulates. The edge either holds its depth or runs shallow, the free leg either stays
extended and turned out or it drops, and the shoulders either keep their check or come round
and take the edge with them. That is the exercise: holding the position.

Watch the free hip. A foot that drops has almost always been given away by a hip that opened
a second earlier.
