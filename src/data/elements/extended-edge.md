A plain edge, held. What makes it an element rather than the gap between two elements is
time: British Ice Skating asks for a third of a circle, or three seconds, before anything
else is allowed to happen. Three seconds is a long while to do nothing on one foot.

There is nothing to hide behind. On a turn a mistake arrives and leaves again; here it
accumulates. The edge either holds its depth or runs shallow, the free leg either stays
extended and turned out or it drops, and the shoulders either keep their check or come
round and take the edge with them. That is the exercise — not the position, the holding of
it.

Watch the free hip rather than the free foot. A foot that drops has almost always been
given away by a hip that opened a second earlier.
