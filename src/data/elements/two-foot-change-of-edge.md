With both feet on the ice, the skater rolls the blades from one pair of edges across the
flat and onto the other pair, so that the curve reverses. No foot leaves the ice and no push
happens: the change is made by moving the body across the blades.

It is the honest test of whether a skater is standing over their skates. Two blades cannot
change edge together unless the weight moves as one thing, so the fault it exposes is a
skater who has been steering with one foot and following with the other. The tracing shows a
long shallow S with a single crossing point, and if the two lines cross at different moments
the feet are not working together.

Skills 1 asks for it in the slalom, where the power changes of edge are exactly this
movement done repeatedly.
