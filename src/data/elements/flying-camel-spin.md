A flying spin whose landing position is a camel: the free leg backwards with the knee higher
than the hip. The ISU names a flying spin by where it lands, so the shape in the air is the
skater's, and its junior short programme for 2026–27 asks for this spin with any air position
and eight revolutions in the camel.

Credit is decided after the landing. If the camel is not reached within the first two
revolutions, the spin loses its flying feature. The harder entry the ISU rewards as a level
feature is a clear butterfly or a near-split in the air, on top of a clear jump.

British Ice Skating asks for it in the National 6 free skate, six revolutions without a change
of foot, and in the National 7 short programme, eight in position.
