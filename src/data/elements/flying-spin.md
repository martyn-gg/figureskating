You jump into it and land already spinning. The ISU names the spin by its landing position,
which may differ from the shape held in the air, and allows no rotation on the ice before the
take-off. A spin that also changes foot or position after a flying start counts as a spin with
a flying entry, a different element, in the ISU's wording and U.S. Figure Skating's.

The fault the panel watches for is the step-over: no clear jump, the free foot stepping across
into the spin. The ISU marks it, and U.S. Figure Skating's synchronised rules add that a three
turn before the jump does not make it a flying spin either.

British Ice Skating allows a flying entry in the spin combination from National 1, and its
National 7 and 8 free skates ask for a flying spin or a spin with a flying entry, six
revolutions or more.
