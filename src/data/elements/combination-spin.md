Two positions, one spin, and the rotation carried through the change. Dropping
into a sit and rising into an upright is the pairing to learn first, and the
order is not arbitrary: a sit holds the free leg stretched forward, well off the
line you are turning about, and an upright stacks everything over the blade. So
the change itself speeds you up.

What earns a position is holding it. Two revolutions each, or it does not count,
and the revolutions you spend arriving somewhere count towards the length of the
spin and not towards the position you are arriving at. British Ice Skating asks
four revolutions in total without a change of foot and six with one.

A camel makes a third, and scores higher for it. The animation here leaves it
out, because this rig cannot honestly draw a free leg changing into or out of a
camel.
