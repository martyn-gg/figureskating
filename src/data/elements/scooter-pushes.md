One foot glides while the other pushes, two or three times in a row, the way a child rides a
scooter, and then the feet change roles. Learn to Skate USA asks for two or three on each foot
at its second level, and New Zealand's KiwiSkate assesses scooter pushes at Tiny Tots 2. Neither
describes the push beyond its name and its count.

Ice Skating Australia's nearest skill is described: the T push. Standing in a T, the front foot
facing forwards and the other at right angles behind it, the skater bends both knees, pushes
with the blade and not the toe, glides on the front foot, then brings the feet together. A
scooter push repeats that push from a glide. Forward stroking, a level later in Learn to Skate
USA, alternates it between the feet.
