You start each stroke with the feet crossed and take the power from the outside edge of the
foot that is becoming the free foot. U.S. Figure Skating's definition adds that the legs cross
above the knees, and its Bronze skating skills test asks for forward cross strokes down one
length of the rink and backward ones down the other.

Above the knee is the distinction that matters. In a crossed step, in front or behind, the legs
cross below it. Ice Skating Australia writes cross rolls and cross strokes as one item in its
Canasta Tango work. U.S. Figure Skating defines them separately and does not say how they
relate, so this guide keeps both pages.

U.S. Figure Skating also asks for them at Adult Silver and adaptive Gold.
