The skating knee folds until the thigh is at least parallel to the ice, and the
free leg reaches forward. That line is the ISU's own definition and it is the
whole of the element: a spin that does not get the thigh down is an upright spin
with a bent knee, and is counted as one.

Getting down is the easier half. The fold carries the hip well behind the line
you are turning about, and the free leg reaching the other way is what balances
it, so the position is held by two things pulling against each other rather than
by everything stacked over the blade.

Rising out of it without letting the rotation die is the rest of the work, and
the diagram shows why it is worth doing well: standing up brings everything to
the axis, so a clean rise is also the fastest part of the spin.
