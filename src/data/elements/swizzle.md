Both blades stay on the ice. Starting heels together, you turn the toes out and press the feet
apart, then turn the toes in and draw them back until they meet, and the two blades trace a
lemon shape. Learn to Skate USA asks for six to eight in a row at its first level, and Ice Skating Australia the same, from a run-up of three to five steps. New Zealand calls it
forward sculling.

Australia's description has three points to hold: the heels and toes meet at the end of each
swizzle, the knees stay slightly bent, and the arms are held out at ninety degrees to the
body. The meeting is the measure, so each swizzle finishes with the feet together. Before it,
Australia teaches the rocking horse, one forward and one backward swizzle movement in place, so
that both feet learn to turn out together.
