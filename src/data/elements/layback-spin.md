The ISU counts the layback as an upright spin, not a position of its own: the skating leg is
straight or nearly so, and what makes it a layback is the upper body, with the head and
shoulders leaning back and the back arched. Where the free leg goes is the skater's choice.
US Figure Skating's rulebook puts the same thing as the head leaning away from the axis the
body turns about, with a definite arch in the back.

The Biellmann, which US Figure Skating describes as the free foot pulled from behind to above
the head, is one of the variations the ISU still counts as upright. The ISU's sideways leaning
spin is the layback's neighbour, with the head and shoulders leaning to the side instead.

British Ice Skating's national tests name the layback among the single-position spins from
National 4. In the ISU's short programme, a women's layback has to hold its position for eight
revolutions.
