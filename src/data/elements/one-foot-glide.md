From a two-foot glide you take one blade off the ice and keep going. The skating knee stays
soft, the free foot stays close and low, and the glide holds its line rather than curving.

Halving the base of support is a much bigger change than it sounds, because a two-foot glide
lets you borrow from whichever foot is winning. On one foot there is nothing to borrow from,
so every wobble is now visible and has to be answered by the ankle. The usual fault is
lifting the free foot high and behind, which throws the shoulders forward to compensate;
carried low and just off the ice, it costs nothing.

Held straight this is a plain glide, and it stays a glide only while the blade is flat. Tip
it either way and you have an edge, which is a different element and the whole of the next
tier of the guide.
