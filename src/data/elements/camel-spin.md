The free leg extends behind and the body folds forward over the skating leg to counter it.
What the ISU asks for is the free knee above hip level, which is worth knowing: the
definition is a knee height and not the flat horizontal line most people picture.

It is the spiral, put on a spot, and everything that makes a spiral difficult makes this
harder. The skater is stretched along a line at right angles to the one they are turning
about, so the shoulders and the free foot each travel a wide circle every revolution while
the hip barely moves at all. A camel a little off centre travels across the ice rather than
wobbling on the spot, and that is why this is the position where centring gets learned.

A spiral that wanders is a camel that will not centre.
