name: Left backward inside push back

Backwards on an inside edge. The skating foot pushes against that edge and you arrive
backwards on the other foot's outside edge, which is the strong one: checked, and able to hold
its curve for as long as you ask.

This draws the tracing a backward stroke draws, because it is the same push. It counts as an
element here because both ends are named. Backward stroking is how you push at all; this is
that push taken from a back inside edge onto a back outside one, which is what the syllabus
asks for and where it asks for it. The guide says they are the same, which is the answer it
also gives for a crossover against a chassé.

British Ice Skating write it both as a push back and as transferring the weight back. They are
one movement.
