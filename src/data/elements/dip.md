From a two-foot glide you bend both knees and sink until the thighs are close to parallel
with the ice, arms and free hands forward, then come back up. The blades stay flat and
parallel throughout and the glide keeps its speed.

The point is not the depth. It is that the ankles bend as much as the knees do — sink by
folding at the knee alone and the hips travel backwards, the weight goes onto the heels and
you sit down. Skating boots resist ankle bend, which is exactly why this is worth practising
as its own thing rather than assuming it will arrive with the sit spin.

Coming back up is the half people neglect, and it is the half that appears later. Every spin
and every jump landing is a controlled rise out of a bent knee.
