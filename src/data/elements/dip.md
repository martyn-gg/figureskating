From a two-foot glide you bend both knees down into a squat, arms stretched out in front, and
come back up while the glide carries on. Ice Skating Australia sets the knees at about ninety
degrees; New Zealand's 2008 KiwiSkate badges put it as hips no higher than the knees. Both
programmes teach it standing still first and then on the move, from three to five steps and a
glide. Learn to Skate USA has it at its first level, with the two-foot glide.

The same depth comes back later on one foot. Australia's lunge asks for the hip no higher than
the skating knee, the teapot holds a low position on one foot, and the ISU defines a sit spin
by the skating thigh being at least parallel to the ice. The dip is that range of knee bend,
learned on two feet.
