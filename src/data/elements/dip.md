From a two-foot glide you bend the knees and ankles and sink until your thighs are close to
parallel with the ice, arms held out in front at shoulder height, then rise again. The blades
stay flat and parallel and the glide keeps going. It is in Learn to Skate USA's first level.

At the bottom you should feel balanced over the middle of the blades, with the weight on
neither the toe nor the heel. The usual fault is bending at the waist: the chest drops towards
the knees, the hips go back, the weight lands on the heels and you sit down on the ice. Keep
the back upright and let the ankles fold as far as the knees do. Skating boots resist that,
which is why it takes practice.

Coming back up is the half people neglect. A jump landing is a controlled rise out of a bent
knee.
