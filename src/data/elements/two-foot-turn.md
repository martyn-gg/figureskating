Both feet stay down and the body turns through a half turn, so that gliding forwards you finish
gliding backwards on the same two blades. Learn to Skate USA asks for it in place at its second
level, and moving on a circle at its third.

It comes from the upper body. You wind the shoulders in the direction of the turn, keep the
feet slightly apart with the weight central, and the skates follow round underneath. It feels
as if the shoulders pull the hips round and the feet arrive last. The usual fault is shoving
the feet round with the shoulders still square: nothing above them is turning, and the rotation
dies halfway. Weight on the toe picks catches them and stops the turn the same way.

The blades scrape as they come round, and that is correct. A blade pointing across its own
travel has no groove to follow.
