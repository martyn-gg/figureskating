Both feet stay down and the body rotates through a half turn, so that a skater gliding
forwards finishes gliding backwards on the same two blades. Learned standing still first,
then on the move, then on a curve.

The turn comes from the upper body and the ice does the rest. You wind the shoulders in the
direction of the turn, keep the feet passive and slightly apart, and let the skates follow
round underneath — pushing the feet round while the shoulders stay square gives a scrape and
a stall. Weight stays central, because a blade with weight on the toe or the heel will grip
and stop the rotation halfway.

It is the first change of direction, and every one-foot turn above it is this turn with a
foot taken away.
