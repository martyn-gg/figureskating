Both feet stay down and the body rotates through a half turn, so that a skater gliding
forwards finishes gliding backwards on the same two blades. Learned standing still first,
then on the move, then on a curve.

It comes from the upper body. You wind the shoulders in the direction of the turn, keep the
feet passive and slightly apart, and let the skates follow round underneath. Shove them round
with the shoulders still square and there is nothing to turn into, so the rotation dies
halfway. Weight stays central, because a blade loaded on the toe or the heel grips and stops
it.

The blades scrape all the way round, and the scrape is the element rather than a fault in it:
a blade pointing across its own travel has no groove to follow.

It is the first change of direction, and every one-foot turn above it is this turn with a
foot taken away.
