Travelling forwards, you turn one or both toes inwards and press the blade flat against the
ice so that it skids, and the scrape takes the speed away. Done on two feet the tracks are
two converging scrapes; on one foot the other blade keeps gliding straight and the stop
happens beside it.

Everything depends on it being a press. The knee bends and drives the blade down into the
ice. If the foot only rotates inwards without weight going through it, the skate carves a
curve and you change direction and keep going. Skaters who cannot stop usually have the
shape right and no pressure in it.

This is the first thing worth learning on the ice and it never stops being used. A skater
without a stop cannot commit to any speed, which quietly limits everything else they try.
