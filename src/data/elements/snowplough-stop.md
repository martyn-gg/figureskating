Travelling forwards, you turn one or both toes inwards and press the blade flat against the
ice so that it skids rather than grips, and the scrape takes the speed away. Done on two
feet the tracks are two converging scrapes; on one foot the other blade keeps gliding
straight and the stop happens beside it.

Everything depends on it being a press and not a turn. The knee bends and drives the blade
down into the ice — if the foot only rotates inwards without weight going through it, the
skate carves a curve and you change direction instead of stopping. Skaters who cannot stop
usually have the shape right and no pressure in it.

This is the first thing worth learning on the ice and it never stops being used. A skater
without a stop cannot commit to any speed, which quietly limits everything else they try.
