The fourth and last of the one-foot turns, and the one with nothing working in its favour. A
3-turn rotates with the lobe and changes edge; a rocker rotates with it and keeps the edge;
a counter fights it and keeps the edge. A bracket fights the curve and changes edge, so both
halves of it are against the skater at once.

What that means in practice is that a bracket cannot be got round with speed. The second
learning objective says as much — controlling the speed to facilitate the turn — which is
unusual in this syllabus, where nearly everything else is assessed on maintaining it. Arrive
too fast and the rotation cannot be produced against the lobe, and the turn becomes a
three with the wrong shape.

Entering from a crossover, as both sides do, means the body is already wound the wrong way
for it. That is deliberate: it puts the skater in the position a step sequence actually
produces, rather than letting them prepare the turn from a long clean edge.
