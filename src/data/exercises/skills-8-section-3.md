Twice the length of the other two and built almost entirely out of changes of foot. A closed
choctaw into an open mohawk, a rocker, a closed mohawk, a counter into another mohawk, a
change of edge, an open choctaw: six ways of arriving on the other foot, one after another,
with crossovers holding it together.

Read as a list that is daunting, and read as skating it is what the whole qualification has
been for. Every one of those has been tested on its own between Skills 4 and Skills 7,
so nothing here is being introduced. What is being asked is whether they can be strung
together at performance speed with a piece of music running underneath, which is a different
question from whether the skater has them.

It ends on the hardest cluster in the syllabus — a rocker into a counter into a double
twizzle — and the skater picks which side to take it on. That choice is the last thing the
National Skills structure asks, and it is a fair one to end on: after eight tests of both
sides equally, the programme lets the skater finish on the side they trust.
