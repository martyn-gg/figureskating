A change of edge is the one element in this test with nothing to see. The blade does not
pivot, the foot does not change, the tracing does not come to a point — it simply crosses
from one side of the blade to the other and the curve goes the other way. That is the whole
difficulty of it, and the reason this guide draws it as a roll rather than a join.

What makes it a *power* change is the knee. The exercise is looking for rhythm and for
speed that survives the length of the rink, and the only source of either is the rise and
fall of the skating knee timed to the change. A slalom done with a stiff leg slows down,
and no amount of edge quality rescues it.

The forward side asks for it on two feet and then on one, and the one-foot section is the
harder half by a distance: on two feet the change can be shared between the blades, and on
one there is nowhere for it to go but the ankle. The backward side stays on two feet
throughout, which is the only reason it sits in Skills 1 at all.
