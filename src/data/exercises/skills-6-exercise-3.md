A loop is the only element in the syllabus that goes nowhere. The foot stays down, the edge
stays the same, the direction of travel stays the same, and the blade draws a small closed
circle inside the lobe it was already on. Everything else here is judged on what the skater
does; a loop is judged on what the ice shows afterwards.

Getting into it is most of the difficulty and the exercise says so — the objective is about
the exit edge of the 3-turn that leads in, not about the loop itself. The turn has to produce
a settled backward edge with the rotation stopped and the free side quiet, because a loop
entered with any rotation left over opens out into a spiral of decreasing radius rather than
closing.

The stationary start is the other constraint. There is no speed to draw the circle with, so
the loop has to be made by the knee and by where the weight sits over the blade, and the
axis is allowed to move to suit the skater and the rink — an unusual concession, and a sign
of how much room a loop actually needs.
