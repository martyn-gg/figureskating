The mirror of exercise 1, and the first learning objective gives away what it is really
about: holding a strong back outside edge in order to perform the choctaw. The turn is not
the assessment. The edge before it is, because a choctaw taken off a weak edge cannot be
placed and has to be jumped into.

That edge is extended, which means the skater arrives at the hardest change of foot in the
test in a position they have been holding rather than one they have just made. It is the
Skills 1 extended edge doing a job four levels later, which is a fair illustration of how
this syllabus is put together.

Coming out, the forward inside counter goes straight into a change of edge. There is no
settled lobe after the turn — the check has to hold only long enough for the edge to be
rolled to the other side of the blade, and if the rotation is still running when that happens
the new edge never takes and the next crossover has nothing to push against.
