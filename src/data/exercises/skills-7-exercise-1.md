A closed choctaw from a forward inside edge is the least comfortable entry in the syllabus.
The skater is on the edge that gives the least to lean against, and the free foot has to come
down behind the skating foot rather than in front of it.
There is no version of this that can be done at the last moment.

The counter into a 3-turn is the other half and it is a genuine conflict of requirements. The
counter has to be checked against the curve, and the 3-turn immediately after it needs that
check released in the direction the curve was going all along. Skaters who can do both turns
separately meet this and find that the second one will not start.

The instruction to repeat down both lengths of the ice is what makes it a Skills 7 exercise
rather than a Skills 5 one. Two clean clusters is not the test; four is, and the fourth is
skated on whatever speed the first three left.
