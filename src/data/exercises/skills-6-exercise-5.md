The longest single phrase in the test: a 3-turn, then a change of edge on the foot the turn
left you on, then a choctaw off the back of it. Three things that each need the body settled,
run together with no glide between them.

The change of edge in the middle is doing the work everyone underestimates. It is what puts
the skater onto the edge the choctaw needs, and it has to happen while the check from the turn
is still being held. Do it early and the check breaks; do it late and the choctaw is placed
off the lobe, which the second learning objective is pointing at when it asks for body
position and edges in preparation for the choctaw.

It then does the same thing on the other foot and ends with a plain 3-turn, which reads like
a formality and is not: it is the last element after a full circuit of the rink, and a tired
3-turn is where the flow the outcome asks about becomes visible.
