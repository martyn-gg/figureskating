Round one circle of a figure eight, in three-beat counts: a forward outside three, a step
back onto the other foot's back outside edge, and a step forward to start again. The second
circle is the same on the other foot, the other way round.

The back outside edge in the middle is the one every jump lands on, and the waltz eight is
often where a skater first holds it on purpose. Coaches look for it to stay on the circle,
with the free leg extended behind and the shoulders still, rather than curling in.

The rhythm keeps the pieces even: each part gets its count, so a rushed turn or a short
edge shows at once.
