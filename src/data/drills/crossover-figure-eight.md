Two circles side by side, about the size of a hockey circle each. You crossover round the
first, change foot where the circles meet, and crossover the other way round the second. The
backward version uses back crossovers and asks the same of the other direction.

Speed has to come from the pushes, not from leaning harder. Each push finishes with the leg
long and the knee straight, and the knee of the skating leg bends and rises in a steady
rhythm. A circle that drifts outward shows the lean is not over the blade.

The change at the centre is where most skaters lose speed and shape: the new circle starts
on the other edge of the other foot, and the body has to turn to face it.
