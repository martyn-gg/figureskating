A cross stroke pushes from the edge of the foot that has just crossed, rather than from the
inside edge behind you. Round a circle, in all four forms, it teaches a push that starts
underneath the body.

The knee does the work: down as the feet cross, the push as it rises. Coaches look for the
rise and fall to be even from stroke to stroke, and for the shoulders to stay level while the
legs cross under them.

The inside and back versions are much harder than the forward outside one, and they are the
ones that build control for the turns and the jump entries that come off those edges.
