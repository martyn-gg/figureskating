Three or four pulls on one foot, a crossover to change foot, then the same on the other, all
the way down the ice. Some coaches add the arms held above the head, which takes them away
as a source of speed and leaves the knee and ankle to do it all.

Each pull is a change of edge on one foot: the knee bends into the curve and rises as the
edge changes, and the drive comes from that rise and fall pressing into the ice. A pull that
does not gain speed is a wiggle.

It is done forwards and backwards, and it builds the leg strength and edge pressure that
spins and jump entries draw on.
