A jump is practised inside a small loop of skating rather than from a standstill: an entry,
the jump, the landing held, a step forward and a cross roll that brings you back to the entry.
The loop is skated again straight away.

Repeating the whole loop teaches the entry as much as the jump, and it puts every take-off in
the same place on the same curve. A skater who varies the entry varies the jump; the pattern
takes that away. Holding the landing until the cross roll also stops the habit of putting the
free foot down early.

The pattern is set for the toe loop and adapts to the Salchow, loop and flip.
