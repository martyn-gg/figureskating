You stroke the length of the rink, crossover round the end, and keep going, using the whole
ice. Part way round you turn backwards, by a two-foot turn at first and later by a three turn
or a mohawk, and do the next lap backwards.

It trains power and flow over distance. Each stroke should glide before the next, and the
crossovers at the ends should carry the speed round rather than scrub it off. Counting
strokes down each side is a plain measure of progress: fewer strokes at the same speed means
each push is doing more.

Rinks that run public sessions usually set the direction of travel, so this is a drill for a
session where that direction suits it.
