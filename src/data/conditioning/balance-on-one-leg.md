Almost everything after the first lessons happens on one foot, on a blade a few
millimetres wide touching the ice along a centimetre or two of its curve. The ankle makes
small corrections all the time, and an ankle that has never had to do it on the floor
does it late on the ice.

Skaters work on this standing on one leg with the knee soft, then with the eyes closed,
then on something that moves under them. The free leg goes where the element puts it:
held behind for a glide, lifted for a spiral.

Ask a coach which way the free side should face for the edge you are working on, and
practise that, because balancing square to nothing in particular is a different
balance.
