Every jump in the guide lands in the same position: one leg bent under the body, the
other stretched behind and turned out, the arms open and the shoulders checked against the
turn that has just finished. A jump that lands and cannot hold that shape for a few
seconds has not really landed.

That makes the landing position worth holding off the ice, on one leg, for longer than a
landing lasts, and stepping into it from a small hop so the knee learns to take the
weight on the way down.

The landing is also where most falls start, so it is worth asking a coach to look at
yours on the floor before it matters on the ice.
