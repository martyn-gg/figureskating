Skating lives on a bent knee. The push comes from it, the edge deepens on it, a turn
rises off it and comes back down onto it, and a sit spin is the same bend taken as far as
it goes. A skater whose knee straightens when they tire loses the edge first.

Off the ice that means the strength to hold a bend for a long time and to go up and down
through it smoothly, on one leg as well as two. The ankle matters as much as the knee: a
boot lets the shin lean forward only so far, and the skater has to use all of it.

Ask a coach or a physiotherapist what depth to aim for. It is not the deepest bend you can
reach but the one you can hold over an edge.
