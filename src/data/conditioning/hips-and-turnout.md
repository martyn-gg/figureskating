Turnout is how far the legs rotate outward from the hip. Skating asks for a lot of it:
the free leg turned out behind on almost every edge, the heels together in a mohawk, and
the spread eagle, which is turnout taken to both feet at once in opposite directions.

Most of a skater's turnout comes from the hip, and forcing it from the knee or the ankle
is the usual way to hurt either. The work off the ice is gradual mobility at the hip, done
warm and never pushed into pain.

How far your hips turn out is partly how they are built. A coach or physiotherapist can
tell you what is realistic, and a spread eagle with less turnout is still a spread
eagle.
